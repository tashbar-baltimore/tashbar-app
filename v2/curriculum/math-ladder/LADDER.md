## How the ladder works
- Math is 10 ladders (strands), side by side. Each ladder has rungs (madregos). Each rung is one real skill, in the order a child's thinking actually grows.
- Each rung has a usual grade, but a boy climbs at his own pace. He can be on a Grade 3 rung in adding and a Grade 2 rung in fractions.
- A short placement check finds his rung on each ladder. He starts there, not at the start of his grade.
- Going up: he passes a rung's check with no help, 2 days apart. Then the next rung opens.
- Going down: if he gets stuck twice, he drops one rung for a short re-teach on the skill underneath, then climbs back. This is built into every rung.
- Every rung is one unit made to the school's standard: a short, warm opening lesson, then hands-on practice games, a "How to do it" box on every question, and a check.
- Old rungs come back in mixed review, so nothing is forgotten.

## Built on
- Building Blocks (Clements & Sarama): learning trajectories, the named levels of thinking for K–2 and early Grade 3. The small grey names under each rung are their level names.
- Clements & Sarama, Learning and Teaching Early Math: the research behind those levels.
- Common Core Progressions (the writers of the standards): the order of skills from Grade 3 to 8, where Building Blocks stops.
- Concrete, then picture, then numbers: every new idea starts with things he can move, then drawings, then numbers.
- Bar models (Singapore math) for story problems, Grades 2–7.
- Short daily number talks and spaced, mixed review.
