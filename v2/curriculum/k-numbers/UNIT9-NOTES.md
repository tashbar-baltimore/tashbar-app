# Kindergarten Numbers, Unit 9 "Parts of 10": notes

**What Esti asked for, in her words:** "keep on getting it done ... immersive, 3D ... upgraded 2.0", and (2026-11-27) "it shouldn't be confusing ... a kid who's 5-6 years old can figure it out." Built to `v2/curriculum/K-CLARITY-RULES.md`.

**Skill (MAP.md):** knows the parts that make 6 to 10, finds the hidden part, and finds more than one way to split a number (K.OA.3, K.OA.4 lead-in). Chant: "Seven is five and two!"

## The look: a calm fruit market, in real 3D
- `market9.js` (three.js, adapted from Unit 8's `kitchen8.js`, same game API): a wooden market table with a soft cloth, round oranges and lemons on a tray in rows of five (so 6–10 is "five and some more"), a big wicker basket that comes down over the hidden part, and two little plates for the two parts.
- Calm, still background: no flying bits, no props. The one exception: a short burst of sparkles when he gets it right (a reward, not a distraction while he works). Mendy and Dovid (painted) stand behind the table.
- The market pictures (order N9) are not here yet: until they come, the background uses the garden panorama and the fruit uses plain colours.
- Phone: the camera moves a little to the right so the basket is never cut off.

## Seven stops, seven ways to play
| Stop | How he plays | New to the Numbers year |
|---|---|---|
| Learn it (opener9) | Seven spills as four oranges and three lemons; two plates: five and two; the bond 7 / 5 / 2; the chant; eight oranges with three under the basket; his turn: seven oranges, two hidden, he shows two on his fingers | parts up to ten |
| Under the basket (hide9) | Count the oranges; the basket comes down over some; he shows the hidden part on his fingers; the basket lifts to check | the hidden part of 6–10 |
| Shake and spill (shake9) | He taps the basket: oranges and lemons spill; he shows each part on its own hand | two parts of 6–10 at once |
| Split it (split9) | Seven (or eight, or ten) fruits; the arrows turn lemons into oranges; the bond shows the parts; each new way goes on the list; a repeated way gets "Let's look" | many ways to make one number |
| Find the mistake (mistake9) | A friend's bond has a wrong number; the fruit shows the truth; he finds the wrong circle and fixes it on his fingers | |
| Review (review9) | Units 6–8: which plate has more (or the same) (Unit 7); the hidden part of 5 and 4 (Unit 8); one more (Unit 6) and one less (Unit 7) with the oranges on the tray, on two hands | |
| Show me (check9) | Mastery: the hidden part of 6, 9 and 10, then two different ways to make seven | |

## Clarity checks (K-CLARITY-RULES)
- One thing to look at: the tray of fruit; the panel holds only the buttons for this round.
- Fruit in rows of five with gaps, so every piece can be counted.
- The hidden fruit is really hidden (not peeking out) and is counted one by one when the basket lifts.
- Numbers are drawn big in white circles in the panel, never on the floor.
- Every wrong move: "Let's look ...", then it shows how, then another try.

## Independent review (round 1) and what changed
A fresh reviewer scored every game. Below 4 were: the fruit cut off on a phone, the two rows overlapping, tiny fruit on the plates, help that did not show how (mistake9, split9, check9), and a review that did not match Units 6–8. All fixed:
- Phone: spilled fruit closer together and centred; rows of five further apart; plate fruit much bigger.
- mistake9: a wrong circle glows while we count what it stands for ("The circle says five. That one is right.").
- split9 and check9: a wrong split gets "Watch", and the hand taps one arrow to a new way; check9 shows a split before his first one.
- opener9: the plates are pointed at as they are named; the chant is call and answer ("Seven is..." he answers "five and two!").
- review9: Unit 6 (one more), Unit 7 (plates, one less) and Unit 8 (parts of 4 and 5), each shown in the demo; the plates have a soft ring.
