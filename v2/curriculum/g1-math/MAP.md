# Grade 1 Math — the full-year map (Tashbar Learning 2.0)

**What Esti asked for (2026-11-26), in her words:** "the year map: read every Grade 1 math lesson in 1.0 and the Common Core Grade 1 math standards; about 14 units, each: skill, chant, 4–5 different games, mastery, review. It must teach everything 1.0 teaches in Grade 1 math, plus what is missing." Grade 1 starts right where Kindergarten Numbers ends. Same Mendy and friends, a year older; new worlds and new games.

**The year:** 15 units and a graduation, about 34 school weeks. Each day is 15–20 minutes; each game is 3–5 minutes.

**Where Kindergarten ends** (`v2/curriculum/k-numbers/MAP.md`): numbers to 20 as "ten and some more", counting to 100 by ones and tens, counting on from any number, adding and taking away within 10, the partners of 10. So Grade 1 does **not** re-teach those. It uses them on day one and goes further.

**Research behind it:** counting on and the "bigger number first" step (Fuson; Carpenter's Cognitively Guided Instruction), the story-problem types (Carpenter, Fennema), making ten and the "cherry" split (Japanese and Singapore textbooks), doubles as anchor facts (Thornton), the equal sign as "the same as" (Falkner, Levi & Carpenter; McNeil), bundling tens and the 120-chart (Kamii; Fuson), the empty number line (Treffers, Dutch Realistic Mathematics), the bar model (Singapore), fluency from strategies, not drilling (Baroody; Boaler), and spaced, mixed review (Dunlosky; Rohrer).

---

## How every game works (the same in every unit)

- **Opener lesson first.** Each unit opens with a short, warm, patient lesson. Mendy shows what we learn and why, and the boy does each step with him.
- **Watch me, now you.** Every game starts with a short demo with voice. Then the boy plays. A big **hear it again** button and a **How to do it** box (pictures and 2–3 short steps, read aloud) are always on screen.
- **Hands on.** He drags, hops, packs, pours, balances, sails and builds in a 3D world. He never just picks A, B or C in practice. Answers he gives as a number, he **writes** on a big number pad (Grade 1 writes numbers; 1.0 only had him tap them).
- **Real things, then pictures, then numbers.** Every new idea is acted out with things in the world, then with dots, frames and lines, then with the number sentence.
- **Spoken lines are 12 words or fewer.** Little reading is needed: the voice says everything, and number sentences are shown big.
- **A gentle fix on every wrong move.** A wrong answer is never just "wrong". The world shows why (the boat sails back, the crates are counted with him, the scale tips) and he tries again.
- **Find the mistake** in every unit: a friend makes a real, common mistake and the boy fixes it.
- **Stars.** Up to 3 per game. 2 stars opens the next game. **Too hard?** After two misses, the game quietly gets easier (smaller numbers, a picture hint, the voice helps). **Too easy?** A bonus round with bigger numbers or a hidden part.
- **Celebrations** last 2 seconds (soft confetti, a chime, Mendy cheers). Then back to learning.

**Every day:**
1. **Warm-up chant** (1 minute). It grows all year: count to 120; by tens; doubles; partners of 10; "ten and some more".
2. **Today's game(s).**
3. **Mixed review round** (1–2 minutes): yesterday's skill, one from a few weeks ago, and one older one.

**Mastery check** at the end of every unit: short items with no help button, mixed from the unit's games. About 7 of 8 right means "mastered". Anything less gives practice games, then the check again.

## Unit 1. Counting to 120 — Orchard Hill — 2 weeks

**Skill:** counts to 120 from any number; reads and **writes** numbers to 120; knows what comes next and before; counts a big group by tens and ones.
**Standard:** 1.NBT.1. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.NUM.L02.01 Count to 120.
**World:** a sunny apple orchard on a hill, with a winding path of stepping stones, a stone wall, and crates of apples.
**Chant:** "Ten, twenty, thirty ... one hundred! One hundred one ... one hundred twenty!" (counting by tens up the hill, then by ones past 100).

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: past 100** | Mendy lights the stones by tens up the hill. Past 100, the boy taps each new stone: 101, 102 ... 110. | After 100, the numbers start again: "one hundred **one**". |
| **Hop ahead** | Mendy stands on 97. "Where will he be after 5 hops?" The boy taps the stone; Mendy hops to check, counting aloud. | Counting on from any number, across 100. Predict first, then check. |
| **Fix the wall** | The orchard wall is a 120 chart. Stones fell out. He drags each stone back into its gap. | The chart's pattern: one more is the next stone; ten more is the stone below. |
| **Crate count** | Crates of 10 apples and some loose apples. He taps the crates (10, 20, 30 ...) and then the apples (31, 32), and **writes** the number. | Counting a big group by tens, then ones. Writing 113, not 10013. |
| **Find the mistake** | Shmuel's stones go "108, 109, 200", or he wrote 1003 for one hundred three. He finds the wrong stone and writes the right number. | The two real mistakes past 100. |

**Mastery check:** 8 items: 2 hop-aheads (one across 100), 2 wall gaps, 2 crate counts (written), 2 "write the number you hear" past 100.
**Slow help:** stay below 100; the stones keep their numbers. **Bonus:** the stones after the start are blank.
**Review:** Kindergarten: teen numbers, counting by tens, adding within 10.

## Unit 2. Count on, count back — The Harbor — 2 weeks

**Skill:** adds by counting on from the bigger number and subtracts by counting back, within 20. Knows the turn-around fact (3 + 8 = 8 + 3).
**Standard:** 1.OA.5, 1.OA.3, 1.OA.6. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.03 Hop On, Hop Back.
**World:** a little harbor: a wooden dock, a row of floating buoys, a sailboat, gulls in the sky.
**Chant:** "Put the big number in your head, and count on!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: the number in your head** | Mendy "puts 8 in his head" (8 fish go into the boat at once), then counts on 9, 10, 11. | No need to count from 1. |
| **Sail on** | The boat is at buoy 9. "Sail 4 more." He **drags** the boat along the buoys; each buoy rings its number. Then he writes where he landed. | Counting on as moving along a line (drag, not tap). |
| **Big one first** | Two cargo nets: 2 and 9. He chooses which net to start from, and the game shows how many counts each way takes. | Start with the bigger number: 9 ... 10, 11. |
| **Turn it around** | A cargo plank with 3 barrels and 8 barrels. He spins the plank; the total stays the same. He writes both facts. | The turn-around fact (commutative property, missing in 1.0). |
| **Sail back** | The boat goes back to the dock: 14, sail back 3. He drags it back and writes the answer. | Counting back to subtract. |
| **Find the mistake** | Dovid adds 5 + 3 by saying "5, 6, 7" (he counted the 5 again). The boy shows the right hops. | The real counting-on mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 8 facts within 20 (5 adding, 3 subtracting), written, with no buoys for the last 3.
**Slow help:** within 10, with the buoys numbered. **Bonus:** start number hidden in a closed crate ("8 inside").
**Review:** Unit 1.

## Unit 3. Number families and fast facts within 10 — The Treehouse Village — 3 weeks

**Skill:** knows the facts within 10 quickly; sees that 3, 4 and 7 are one family (3 + 4 = 7, 4 + 3 = 7, 7 − 3 = 4, 7 − 4 = 3); solves subtraction by thinking addition.
**Standard:** 1.OA.4, 1.OA.6 (fluency within 10), 1.OA.8. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.06 Missing Piece (within 10).
**World:** treehouses in big friendly trees, rope bridges, a basket lift on a pulley.
**Chant:** a call-and-answer: "Seven is ..." "Three and four!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: one family** | Three numbers live in one treehouse. Mendy shows the 4 facts they make. | Adding and taking away are one family. |
| **Basket lift** | 7 acorns. Some go up in the basket out of sight. He sees 3 left and **writes** how many went up. | The hidden part: subtraction as a missing addend. |
| **Family house** | He drags number blocks to build all 4 sentences of a family on the treehouse walls. | Fact families. |
| **Rope-bridge relay** | Quick facts within 10. Each right answer lays a plank; Mendy crosses the bridge. A picture-help button shows the ten-frame. | Speed from understanding (no timer pressure: the bridge just grows). |
| **Find the mistake** | Eli says 9 − 4 = 6 because "4 + 6 = 9". The boy checks with the basket and fixes it. | Checking subtraction with adding. |

**Mastery check:** 10 facts within 10 (mixed), 8 right without help; plus 2 hidden-part items.
**Slow help:** families of 5 and 6 first. **Bonus:** families of 10.
**Review:** Units 1–2.

## Unit 4. Doubles and near doubles — Mirror Lake — 2 weeks

**Skill:** knows doubles to 10 + 10 and uses them for near doubles (6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1).
**Standard:** 1.OA.6. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.04 Doubles.
**World:** a still mountain lake that shows everything twice; ducks, lily pads, a little wooden pier.
**Song:** the doubles song: "Double 6 is 12, like a box of eggs ..." (real-life doubles: a box of 12 eggs, 2 hands of 5, a week and a week).

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: the lake doubles it** | Mendy puts 4 ducks on the shore; the lake shows 4 more. "Double 4 is 8." | A double is the same number twice. |
| **Lily-pad double** | He puts ducks on the pier; the reflection appears; he **writes** the total. | Building doubles. |
| **Double plus one** | A double, then one more frog jumps on (6 + 7). He finds the double hiding inside, then adds one. | Near doubles. |
| **Double catch** | Doubles facts float by as boats. He steers a net to catch the boat whose double matches the number on the dock. | Recall (steering, not tapping). |
| **Find the mistake** | Yossi says 7 + 8 = 14 ("double 7"). The boy adds the forgotten one. | Near doubles need the extra one. |

**Mastery check:** 8 items: 4 doubles, 4 near doubles, written.
**Slow help:** doubles to 5 + 5 with the reflection. **Bonus:** "double minus one" (7 + 6 = 7 + 7 − 1).
**Review:** Units 2–3.

## Unit 5. Make a ten to add — The Fair Carousel — 3 weeks

**Skill:** adds within 20 by making a ten: 8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3.
**Standard:** 1.OA.6. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.05 Make Ten.
**World:** a bright family fair with a carousel of 10 horses, a ticket booth, bunting.
**Chant:** "Fill the ten, then the rest!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: fill the carousel** | 8 boys ride; 5 more come. 2 fill the carousel, 3 wait. 10 and 3 is 13. | Making ten. |
| **Carousel fill** | He drags boys from the line onto the empty horses until it is full, then writes "10 + ?" and the total. | The ten-frame as a carousel. |
| **Cherry split** | He splits the second number into two cherries: the part that makes ten, and the rest. | The Japanese "cherry" split (number bond). |
| **Ten-ticket pairs** | Tickets come down a chute; he slides each one to its "partner of 10" slot. | Partners of 10, fast (they are needed for making ten). |
| **Find the mistake** | Mendy says 9 + 4 = 10 + 4. The boy shows that one of the 4 moved: 10 + 3. | The real make-ten mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 8 facts with 7, 8 or 9 plus a number, written; 2 cherry splits.
**Slow help:** only 9 + something first. **Bonus:** 7 + 6 by making ten from either number.
**Review:** Units 3–4.

## Unit 6. Taking away within 20 — The Balloon Festival — 3 weeks

**Skill:** subtracts within 20 by taking away, counting back, going back through ten (14 − 6 = 14 − 4 − 2), and thinking addition.
**Standard:** 1.OA.4, 1.OA.6. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.02 Block Quest.
**World:** a hot-air balloon field at sunrise; baskets, sandbags, little balloons on strings.
**Chant:** "Back to ten, then back some more!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: back to ten** | 14 balloons; 6 float away: first 4 (back to 10), then 2 more. | Going back through ten. |
| **Let them float** | He taps the knots to let balloons go, as many as the story says, and writes how many are left. | Take away, acted out. |
| **Down the ladder** | The basket ladder is a number line. He climbs down: first to 10, then the rest. | Counting back in two jumps. |
| **Think addition** | "13 − 9: what goes with 9 to make 13?" He fills sandbags until the basket balances. | Subtraction as a missing addend. |
| **Find the mistake** | Shmuel says 15 − 7 = 9 (he counted back from 15 including 15). The boy fixes it on the ladder. | The real counting-back mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 8 subtraction facts within 20, written; 2 with no picture.
**Slow help:** within 10, then teen minus a small number. **Bonus:** subtract across ten with no ladder.
**Review:** Units 4–5.

## Unit 7. Adding within 20, choosing a way, and three numbers — Lighthouse Island — 3 weeks

**Skill:** adds within 20 using the best way (count on, double, make ten); adds three numbers by finding a ten or a double first.
**Standard:** 1.OA.2, 1.OA.3, 1.OA.6. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.OPS.L02.01 Twenty Trail, MATH.OPS.L02.07 Three in a Row.
**World:** a small island with a striped lighthouse, tide pools, a treasure path of shells.
**Chant:** "Look for a ten, look for a double!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: which way?** | Mendy and Eli add 9 + 6 two ways and both get 15. | Many good ways, one answer. |
| **Three treasure chests** | Three chests (7, 5, 3). He pairs the two that make ten first, then adds the third, and writes the total. | Three numbers (1.OA.2), any order (1.OA.3). |
| **Way finder** | A fact appears on the lighthouse; he sends it down the path for "count on", "double" or "make ten", then solves it. | Choosing a strategy. |
| **Lighthouse climb** | Mixed facts within 20; every right answer lights one step up the lighthouse. | Mixed practice to fluency. |
| **Find the mistake** | Avi adds 4 + 6 + 4 and forgets one number. The boy finds the missing chest. | Three numbers: count each once. |

**Mastery check:** 6 facts within 20 and 2 three-number sums, written.
**Slow help:** three numbers with a ten pair always present. **Bonus:** four numbers.
**Review:** Units 5–6.

## Unit 8. The equal sign and the missing number — The Market Scales — 2 weeks

**Skill:** knows that = means "the same as"; tells if 6 + 2 = 5 + 3 is true; finds the missing number anywhere (8 + ? = 13, ? − 3 = 9).
**Standard:** 1.OA.7, 1.OA.8. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.OPS.L02.08 Balance Scale, MATH.OPS.L02.06 Missing Piece.
**World:** a market square with a big golden balance scale, fruit stalls (kosher fruit), baskets.
**Chant:** "Equal means the same on both sides!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: the same on both sides** | Mendy puts 6 + 2 apples on one side and 5 + 3 on the other. The scale stays level. | = means the same as, not "the answer comes next". |
| **True or tip** | A number sentence goes onto the scale; he says if it balances, then watches it settle. | True and false sentences (including 7 = 7 and 9 = 4 + 5). |
| **Balance it** | One side has a gap. He drags fruit in until the scale is level, then writes the missing number. | The unknown in any place. |
| **Mystery bag** | "A bag of apples and 3 more make 9." He opens the bag after he writes his guess. | Start-unknown problems. |
| **Find the mistake** | Dovid writes 4 + 5 = 9 + 2 = 11 (a chain). The boy shows the scale tipping. | The real equal-sign mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 4 true/false sentences and 4 missing numbers in all places.
**Slow help:** missing number at the end first. **Bonus:** missing number on the left: ? = 4 + 7.
**Review:** Units 6–7.

## Unit 9. Story problems within 20 — Shabbos Street — 3 weeks

**Skill:** solves every kind of story problem within 20: adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing ("how many more"), with the unknown in every place; stories with three numbers.
**Standard:** 1.OA.1, 1.OA.2. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.OPS.L02.09 Story Problems (plus the kinds 1.0 was missing: change-unknown take-from, compare with the bigger number unknown, three-number stories).
**World:** a warm street on Erev Shabbos: houses with flower boxes, a bakery with challos, a shul, boys carrying packages.
**Chant:** "Read it, act it, draw it, solve it!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: act it out** | A story is told; Mendy and friends act it out on the street; then the number sentence appears. | Story first (Carpenter). |
| **Story stage** | He acts out each story with the characters and things, then writes the answer. | All join and separate kinds. |
| **Story strip** | He builds the story as a bar (Singapore bar model): two parts and a whole, or two bars to compare. | Seeing what is missing. |
| **How many more?** | Two lines of boys at the bakery; he pairs them up to see who has more, then writes how many more. | Compare stories (1.0's weakest kind). |
| **Find the mistake** | "Eli has 5 more challos than Ari, who has 8." Shloimy says 3. The boy acts it out. | The real compare mistake ("more" does not always mean add). |

**Mastery check:** 8 stories, one of each kind, written.
**Slow help:** the voice tells the story twice and the characters stay on the street. **Bonus:** two-step stories.
**Review:** Units 7–8.

## Unit 10. Tens and ones — The Sukkah Builders — 3 weeks

**Skill:** knows a two-digit number is tens and ones; 10 ones make a ten; 40 is 4 tens; teens are 1 ten and some ones; builds and reads numbers to 120.
**Standard:** 1.NBT.2. **1.0 lesson:** MATH.PV.L02.01 Tens and Ones.
**World:** a yard in Elul where boys build a sukkah: bundles of schach poles, boards, a big cart.
**Chant:** "Ten ones make a ten! Tie it up!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: tie a bundle** | Mendy counts 10 poles and ties them: now it is one ten. | Ten ones are one ten. |
| **Bundle up** | Loose poles lie around. He ties bundles of ten, then writes how many tens, how many ones, and the number. | Making tens from ones (Kamii). |
| **Load the cart** | "Load 47." He drags 4 bundles and 7 poles onto the cart. | Building a number from its digits. |
| **Number swap** | 36 and 63 carts side by side. He shows which has more tens. | The place of a digit matters. |
| **Find the mistake** | Yossi loads 5 bundles and 2 poles for "25". The boy swaps them. | The real place-value mistake. |

**Mastery check:** build 4 numbers, read 4 bundles-and-poles groups.
**Slow help:** numbers to 50. **Bonus:** to 120 ("12 tens").
**Review:** Units 8–9.

## Unit 11. Bigger, smaller, and 10 more, 10 less — The Mountain Cable Car — 2 weeks

**Skill:** compares two-digit numbers with >, = and <; finds 10 more and 10 less in his head.
**Standard:** 1.NBT.3, 1.NBT.5. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.PV.L02.02 Alligator Signs, MATH.PV.L02.03 Jump Ten.
**World:** a snowy-white mountain (sunny, gentle) with a cable car that stops at a station every ten.
**Chant:** "Tens first! Then the ones!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: tens first** | Two cable cars carry 42 and 38 boxes. Mendy compares the tens first. | Compare tens, then ones. |
| **Seesaw signs** | Two numbers on a seesaw; it tips toward the bigger one, and he drags the right sign (> < =) into the middle. | The sign opens to the bigger number (instead of 1.0's alligator). |
| **Ride ten up** | The cable car rides up one station: 34 becomes 44. He writes 10 more, then 10 less. | 10 more and 10 less change only the tens. |
| **120-chart elevator** | A tall chart tower; he rides the elevator up and down by tens to reach a number. | Jumps of ten on the chart. |
| **Find the mistake** | Eli says 29 > 31 "because 9 is big". The boy compares the tens. | The real comparing mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 4 comparisons with signs, 4 "10 more / 10 less" items.
**Slow help:** numbers with different tens first. **Bonus:** the same tens, different ones (47 and 43).
**Review:** Units 9–10.

## Unit 12. Adding and taking away tens and ones to 100 — The Harvest Market — 3 weeks

**Skill:** adds a two-digit number and a one-digit number, or a multiple of 10, **including making a new ten** (38 + 5); subtracts tens (80 − 30).
**Standard:** 1.NBT.4, 1.NBT.6. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.OPS.L02.10 Add Tens, MATH.OPS.L02.11 Take Tens (plus making a new ten, which 1.0 left out).
**World:** a harvest market with crates of ten apples, pumpkins, a weighing table, carts.
**Chant:** "Tens with tens, ones with ones!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: tens with tens** | Mendy adds 34 + 20: the crates go together, the loose apples wait. | Add tens to tens. |
| **Crate stack** | He adds crates and apples to a stall and writes the total. | Two-digit plus tens, plus ones. |
| **A new crate** | 38 + 5: he fills the open crate (2 apples), a new crate closes, 3 are left: 43. | Making a new ten (1.NBT.4, missing in 1.0). |
| **Market day** | Crates of ten are sold: 80 − 30. He takes crates off the cart. | Subtracting tens (1.NBT.6). |
| **Number-line hops** | He adds on an empty number line with jumps of ten and jumps of one. | The empty number line (Dutch method). |
| **Find the mistake** | Avi writes 38 + 5 = 313. The boy shows the new crate. | The real regrouping mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 8 items: 3 tens-plus, 3 with a new ten, 2 tens-minus, written.
**Slow help:** no new ten first. **Bonus:** two-digit plus two-digit without regrouping.
**Review:** Units 10–11.

**World:** a friendly building yard: planks, toy diggers, a measuring table, a big chalkboard chart.
**Chant:** "End to end, no gaps, no bumps!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: fair measuring** | Mendy measures a plank with cubes, with gaps and then the right way. | Units with no gaps or overlaps. |
| **Line them up** | He drags three planks into order, shortest to longest; then uses a string to compare two that are far apart. | Ordering and indirect comparing (1.MD.1). |
| **Cube measure** | He lays cubes end to end along a board and writes how long it is. | Length as a count of units. |
| **Tally and chart** | Boys vote for a Shabbos treat; he makes tally marks and a picture chart, then answers "how many more" and "how many in all". | Data with three groups. |

## Unit 14. Telling time and coins — Clock Tower Town — 3 weeks

**Skill:** tells and sets time to the hour and half hour on a clock with hands, and on a digital clock; knows the penny, nickel, dime and quarter and counts small amounts (to 60 cents), starting with the biggest coin.
**Standard:** 1.MD.3 (time). Coins are a Grade 2 standard (2.MD.8) that 1.0 teaches in Grade 1, so we keep them. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.MEAS.L02.02 Clock to the Half Hour, MATH.MEAS.L02.03 Coins.
**World:** a little town square with a tall clock tower, a shul, a school, a home, and a tzedakah box by the door.
**Chant:** "The short hand tells the hour, the long hand tells the minutes!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: two hands** | Mendy shows the short hand and the long hand; at half past, the short hand is halfway. | Both hands matter. |
| **Set the tower** | "Set it to half past 4." He turns the hands with his finger. | Setting time, not only reading it. |
| **Mendy's day** | Pictures of Mendy's day (Shacharis, school, lunch, Mincha, supper, bed) go on the clock in order with their times. | Time in real life, in order. |
| **Tzedakah box** | He drags coins into the box to make an amount, biggest coins first. | Coin values and counting on (by 25s, 10s, 5s, 1s). |
| **Find the mistake** | Eli reads 6:30 as "half past 7" because the short hand is near 7. The boy fixes it. | The real half-hour mistake. |

**Mastery check:** 4 times to read or set, 4 coin amounts.

## Unit 15. Shapes and fair shares — The Art Studio — 2 weeks

**Skill:** knows what makes a shape a shape (sides and corners, not colour, size or turning); builds bigger shapes from smaller ones, flat and solid; cuts circles and rectangles into halves and fourths (quarters), and knows more pieces means smaller pieces.
**Standard:** 1.G.1, 1.G.2, 1.G.3. **1.0 lessons:** MATH.GEO.L02.01 Shape Rules, MATH.GEO.L02.02 Halves and Quarters (plus building shapes and solids, and the word "fourths", which 1.0 left out).
**World:** a bright art studio with an easel, clay, blocks, and a pizza oven for a fair-share party (dairy pizza).
**Chant:** "Equal parts, fair shares! Two halves, four fourths!"

| Game | What he does | The trick |
|---|---|---|
| **Opener: what makes a triangle?** | Mendy turns, stretches and colours a triangle; it is still a triangle. A shape with a gap is not. | Defining attributes. |
| **Shape rules** | He sorts shapes into "is a triangle" and "is not" by sides and corners, even when big, small or turned. | Attributes that count and those that don't. |
| **Build it** | He drags pattern blocks to make a hexagon, a house, a bigger triangle; then stacks solids into a tower. | Composing shapes (1.G.2). |
| **Fair shares** | He slices pizzas and cakes into 2 or 4 equal parts and names a half, a fourth, a quarter. | Equal shares (1.G.3). |
| **Find the mistake** | Yossi cut a pizza into 4 parts that are not equal and calls them fourths. The boy cuts it fairly. | "Equal" matters. |

**Mastery check:** 3 shape-rule items, 2 builds, 3 fair-share items.
**Slow help:** halves first. **Bonus:** which is bigger, a half or a fourth of the same pizza?
**Review:** Units 13–14.

## Graduation — 1 week (1.0's Mixed Review, MATH.MIX.L02.01)

## What 2.0 adds that 1.0 was missing

1. **Writing numbers** to 120 (1.NBT.1): he writes on a number pad; 1.0 only had him tap.
2. **Turn-around facts** taught on their own (1.OA.3), in Unit 2.
3. **Fluency within 10** (1.OA.6), with a full unit of number families and fast facts (Unit 3).
4. **All the story-problem kinds** (1.OA.1), including take-from with the change unknown and compare with the bigger number unknown, and **three-number stories** (1.OA.2) (Unit 9).
5. **Making a new ten** when adding to 100 (1.NBT.4): 38 + 5 (Unit 12).
6. **Tally charts and "how many in all"** (1.MD.4), in Unit 13.
7. **Building shapes and solids** (1.G.2) and the word **fourths**, plus "more parts means smaller parts" (1.G.3), in Unit 15.
8. A **find the mistake** game in every unit, using the real mistakes Grade 1 boys make.

## Why 15 units and not 14
Grade 1 has more separate skills than Kindergarten, and 1.0 also teaches coins. Fitting them into 14 would have joined subtraction with addition strategies, which is too much for one unit. Each unit is still 2–3 weeks.
