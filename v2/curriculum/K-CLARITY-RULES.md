# Kindergarten clarity rules (Shapes and Numbers, 2.0)

**What Esti asked for, in her words (2026-11-27):** "something a 5-6-year-old can figure out and learn, and it shouldn't be confusing." Plain flat shapes first; real things only later; every point gets its marker; no busy backgrounds, silly 3D or butterflies; comparisons a child can't argue about.

Every K game screen is checked against the numbered rules below. A rule marked **(test)** is checked by a script; the rest are checked by playing the game and looking.

## Why (the research, short)
- **Clements & Sarama, learning trajectories (geometry).** Young children first know shapes as whole pictures ("it looks like a door"), and many think a triangle must sit on its flat side and be "pointy and even". They move on only when they see *many* examples (sizes, colours, turns, skinny and wide) next to close non-examples, and when an adult points at the parts (sides, corners). Pictures with extra detail pull attention to the wrong thing.
- **van Hiele levels.** Level 0 (visual): a child names a shape by how it looks. Level 1 (descriptive): he knows it by its parts ("three sides, three corners"). Kindergarten moves from 0 to the start of 1, so the shape must be clean enough that its parts are the only thing to see.
- **Common Core K.G.1–6.** Name shapes regardless of orientation or size (K.G.2); tell flat (2D) from solid (3D) (K.G.3); compare shapes by sides, corners and equal lengths (K.G.4); describe objects in the world with shape words and position words (K.G.1).
- **Clements & Sarama, learning trajectories (number); K.CC, K.OA, K.MD.** Counting with one-to-one tagging (each thing touched once, the last number says how many) needs every thing to count to be separate and visible. Comparing "more / less" (K.CC.6) starts with sets that look clearly different. Measurement (K.MD.1–3) starts with direct comparison of one attribute where the answer is obvious, before any trick.
- **NCTM / attention research (cognitive load, "seductive details").** Decorations and motion that are not the lesson lower young children's learning. One focus at a time; the thing to tap is the strongest thing on screen.

## The rules

### A. Shapes
1. **First meeting = plain flat shape.** The first lesson for each shape shows it flat, face-on, one solid colour, nothing painted on it (no texture, object, Lego, basket, drum). **(test: lesson pieces use plain paint in the first game of each shape)**
2. **Then many examples.** Different sizes, colours and turns (a triangle on its point is still a triangle), mixed with close non-examples (oval vs circle, leaning shape vs rectangle).
3. **Real things only after that.** "A tire is a circle" comes in a later game, with the plain shape glowing on top of the object.
4. **Every corner gets a marker.** When the game talks about corners, every corner gets its own ball, and they are counted one by one. Never only some. **(test: number of corner markers = number of corners)**
5. **Every side lights.** When the game talks about sides, every side lights up in turn. Never only some.
6. **Solids look like solids** (sphere, cube, cylinder, cone), clearly different from flat shapes, and are named only after the flat shapes are known.

### B. Comparing and counting
7. **Comparisons are obvious.** Heavier/lighter, longer/shorter, bigger/smaller, more/less only between things where the answer is clear to a child. "Big is not always heavy" only with something every child knows is light (balloon, feather) against something every child knows is heavy (rock).
8. **Things to count are separate and clear.** No overlapping, no hidden ones, no decoration that looks like one more.

### C. The screen
9. **One thing to look at.** The thing he taps is the biggest, brightest thing on the screen.
10. **Calm background.** Soft and still: no butterflies, no flying or falling bits, no silly props, nothing moving that is not the game. **(test: calm worlds have no extra props)**
11. **Words match pictures.** "Cupcakes" only where there are cupcakes.
12. **Numbers and words are big and face him**, from every camera angle (stood up if on the ground).
13. **The Play button and the title are always on screen. Every button is at least 64 px** on a Chromebook (1366×768) and a phone (390×844). **(test)**

### D. The voice
14. **12 words or fewer per line.** Warm and simple. **(test)**
15. **First try is shown:** "Watch me ... Now you!"
16. **A wrong move gets kind help that shows how,** then another try.

## When in doubt
Choose what a kindergarten teacher would choose: fewer things, bigger things, plainer things, clearer words.
