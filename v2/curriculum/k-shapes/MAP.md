# Kindergarten Shapes, Patterns and Measuring: the year plan

**Esti asked for:** "Make the year plan ... the full year ... Over the top, 3D, immersive, clear, very kid friendly, based on research."
**The goal (check every game against it):** fun, easy, skill-building games that teach every kind of boy real skills, one step on top of the next, all year, until he graduates.

## How every game works (the same in every unit)
- **3D and hands-on.** Glossy 3D toy pieces in a warm room. He drags, taps, turns and builds. Nothing to read: every word is spoken, with a "hear it again" button.
- **Learn → watch → try.** A short lesson with a 3D model (count sides and corners together, then **he** taps them). A hand shows one move ("Watch me"). Then he does it.
- **A chant on a beat** for every new shape or idea. He claps along.
- **Mistakes teach.** A wrong piece is explained by its rule (its corners light up and are counted), then he tries again.
- **Help and challenge.** After 2 mistakes the rule is taught again. If he does nothing, the hand points the way. A quick boy gets a bonus round of tricky ones.
- **Find the mistake.** Every game ends with a friend (Dovid, Ari, Shloimy, Eli, Tzvi...) who makes a mistake for him to catch.
- **Stars.** 2 of 3 stars opens the next game.
- **Short.** One game takes 3–5 minutes. A day is 2–3 games.
- **Shapes in every turn and size.** Never only the "usual" picture. Measuring compares things side by side, with no rulers.

## Why this order (the research, in one line each)
- **Shapes before patterns, sorting and measuring.** Children first know a shape by how it looks, then by its rules (sides and corners). We teach the rules early, so "turned is still a square" makes sense. (Clements & Sarama, learning trajectories; van Hiele)
- **Look-alikes teach the rule.** An oval is not a circle; a curvy "triangle" is not a triangle. (Clements, Swaminathan, Hannibal & Sarama, 1999)
- **Building shapes from shapes** grows spatial skill. (Levine et al., 2012; Verdine et al., 2014)
- **Patterns:** find the part that repeats, then copy, continue, fix and make. (Rittle-Johnson and colleagues)
- **Measuring:** line things up from the same start before anything else. (Szilágyi, Clements & Sarama, 2013)
- **Old skills come back** in mixed review rounds, because spaced practice lasts. (Cepeda et al., 2006; Rohrer & Taylor, 2007)

## What "mastered" means (the check at the end of each unit)
- **Unit 1:** names all four flat shapes in any turn or size.
- **Unit 2:** counts the sides and corners of any of those four, and says the shape.
- **Unit 3:** picks the real shape from look-alikes; knows a hexagon.
- **Unit 4:** continues an AB and an ABB pattern.
- **Unit 5:** names the four solid shapes and says flat or solid.
- **Unit 6:** fills a picture outline with shapes.
- **Unit 7:** puts a block where he hears.
- **Unit 8:** continues an ABC pattern and fixes a broken one.
- **Unit 9:** sorts by a rule and says which group has more.
- **Unit 10:** compares two lengths fairly.
- **Unit 11:** compares two weights on a balance.
