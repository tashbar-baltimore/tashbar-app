# The graphics prompt for Tashbar Learning 2.0 (paste this into any builder's instructions)

THE LOOK: CUTTING-EDGE 3D, LIKE THE NEWEST CHILDREN'S GAMES

Every game in 2.0 must look like it was made by a top team of game artists. The goal: a boy sees the screen and can't wait to touch it. This is binding on every game.

1. REAL 3D, NEVER FLAT.
   - Build every scene in real 3D (WebGL, using three.js). Letters, nekudos, houses, people, trees and toys are 3D objects with depth, light and shadow.
   - Flat drawings, clip-art and plain shapes on a plain background are not allowed.

2. THE STYLE: "premium toy-box 3D".
   - Soft, rounded, chunky shapes, like the best new mobile games for children.
   - Glossy, candy-like letters with a beveled edge, a shine and a soft shadow on the ground.
   - Bright, rich, happy colors (never washed out or grey), all from one shared palette.
   - Soft sunlight from one side, gentle sky light, real reflections and soft ground shadows.

3. A LIVING WORLD.
   - Every scene is a place: grassy hills, trees, flowers, drifting clouds, the sun, a house, a shul, a sukkah, a Shabbos table.
   - Something always moves gently: clouds drift, the camera sways a little, letters float.
   - Motion must never pull attention away from the letter being taught.

4. CHARACTERS WITH LIFE.
   - Boys and family members are 3D, round and friendly. Boys wear a kippah, tzitzis and modest clothing.
   - They blink, wave, hop and react: they cheer when he's right, and look thoughtful when he's wrong.
   - The art is all original. Never copy characters, art or music from any other app, show or game.

5. THE LETTER IS THE STAR.
   - The letter being taught is the biggest, shiniest, best-lit thing on the screen.
   - Letters use the exact shape of a real Hebrew font, made 3D.
   - Nekudos are glossy 3D dots and bars, placed exactly right.
   - The difference between look-alike letters glows in color.

6. TOUCH FEELS ALIVE.
   - Everything he can touch reacts: it grows a little when touched, follows his finger smoothly, and bounces when it lands.
   - A right answer gets a burst of 3D confetti and sparkles, a happy chime, and the house or character jumps for joy. This lasts at most 2 seconds.
   - A wrong answer gets a gentle shake, a glowing circle on the thing to notice, and a kind voice explaining. It is never scary.

7. BUTTONS LIKE CANDY.
   - Buttons are big (at least 64 pixels), round and glossy, and press down when touched.
   - The "hear it again" button is always in the same corner.
   - No menus and no pop-ups.

8. IT MUST RUN EVERYWHERE.
   - Every game must run smoothly on a school Chromebook (1366×768) and on a phone, and load quickly on slow Wi-Fi.
   - Use low-detail 3D models, small textures, and no video.
   - If a device can't show 3D, show a clean, simple version instead of a blank screen.

9. CHECK IT WITH YOUR EYES.
   - Before showing anything, take pictures of every screen at Chromebook size and phone size, and look at them.
   - If any screen looks flat, dull, washed out, crowded, cut off or blank, it is not finished.

10. VALUES.
    - Warm, modest, frum family life: Tatty, Mommy, Bubby, Zeidy, shul, Shabbos, Yom Tov, a mezuzah on the door.
    - Never write the Divine Name.
    - Celebrations are short, then straight back to learning.

The test for every screen: would a 5-year-old boy point at it and say "WOW, I want to play that!"? If not, make it better.

---
Note for builders: the art style belongs to all of 2.0, not only Kriyah. Once it's approved, this belongs in the shared art and sound kit through its own small pull request.
