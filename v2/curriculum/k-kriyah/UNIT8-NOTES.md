The middle stays clear for the letters. A painted shul background is ordered (`art-requests.md`).

## Decisions I made on my own (each one line, so you can say yes or change it)
1. **Mendy tells the story, not Moshe.** Moshe has no painted picture yet, and the 3D people were taken out (HoS 2026-11-26). Mendy and Tatty already have painted pictures.
2. **The picture words are shofar, Simchas Torah (a flag) and Torah:** each starts with its letter's sound in English and in Hebrew.
3. **Suf has no picture.** It comes from Tuf losing its dot, the way Veis, Chaf and Fei came.
4. **Shin's dot is "on the right head", Sin's "on the left head".** Right and left are as the boy sees the screen.
5. **The Torah is treated with respect:** it is never spun, shrunk or "turned into" a letter; the letter rises beside it.
6. **"Now you know the whole Alef-Beis!"** in the story, though the final letters (Unit 9) are still to come: they are the same letters in their "Shabbos clothes".
7. **The look-alike for Tuf is Ches** (Tuf has a little foot), with Hei as the third box.
8. **The review is a Letter Hunt of Unit 7** (Kuf, Reish), in the sukkah (Simchas Torah is the day after Sukkos).

## For the rebbe (every sound and rule used in Unit 8)
- **Letters:** Shin (שׁ) says "sh"; Sin (שׂ) says "s"; Tuf (תּ) says "t"; Suf (ת) says "s". Lone sounds, never with a vowel.
- **Dots:** Shin's dot is on its right head, Sin's on its left head. Tuf has a dot (dagesh) inside; without it, it is Suf and says s.
- **Same sounds:** Sin and Suf say s, like Samech; Tuf says t, like Tes. These are never asked side by side.
- **Look-alikes:** Hei's leg doesn't touch the roof; Ches's leg touches the roof, with no foot; Tuf has a little foot at the bottom.
- **Words:** shofar, Simchas Torah, Torah, shul, aron kodesh (in the picture only).
- **Chant order (the whole Alef-Beis):** Alef, Beis, Veis, Gimmel, Daled, Hei, Vov, Zayin, Ches, Tes, Yud, Kaf, Chaf, Lamed, Mem, Nun, Samech, Ayin, Pei, Fei, Tzadik, Kuf, Reish, Shin, Sin, Tuf, Suf.
- **Temporary:** the letter sounds are the computer voice until the rebbe's recording is in ("shh", "suh", "tuh").
