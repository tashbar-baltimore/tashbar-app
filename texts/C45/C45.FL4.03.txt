Dear Yaakov,

Ima says I should write to you every week, so here is my first letter from Baltimore. It is long, because a lot happened today.

My new school is big. My class has twenty-two boys, and my rebbe, Rabbi Feldman, is kind and very smart. This morning he brought in an esrog from Eretz Yisrael, in a silver box. It was the most beautiful esrog I have ever seen, even nicer than the ones at your Abba's store.

Before recess, the rebbe asked me to bring his magnifying glass to the office. The office was locked, so I waited on the bench outside it. When the bell rang, I walked back up the stairs, holding the glass behind my back so nobody would think I took it.

When I came into the room, everybody was staring at me. The silver box was empty. The esrog was gone!
===
One boy, Tzvi Hirsch, said it out loud: he thinks I took it. My face got so hot. I did not know what to say, so I said nothing at all. Was that the right thing to do? I still do not know.

But Yaakov, not everyone thinks I did it. A boy named Yehuda keeps a little blue notebook of clues. He looked at me, and then he looked at the folded cloth in the box, and he did not say a word against me. Another boy, Avrumi, drew a map of the whole classroom. I think they are trying to find out what really happened.

I miss you. I miss the snow on Mont Royal, and Bubby's soup, and our shul on Sunday mornings. Please write back soon and tell me everything.

Your cousin,
Nosson

P.S. If you find out how to make friends in a new school, please tell me fast!
--- words
magnifying: making something look bigger than it really is
locked: shut with a key so nobody can open it
staring: looking at someone for a long time without stopping
clues: small facts that help you solve a mystery
cousin: the child of your aunt or uncle
