title: The Case of the Missing Shofar

On the morning of Rosh Chodesh Elul, Rabbi Feldman opened the closet at the back of the classroom, and his face went pale. The shelf where the class shofar always sat, wrapped in a blue velvet bag, was empty.

"Boys," he said, "has anyone seen the shofar?"

Twenty-two heads shook. Nobody had.

"We need it tomorrow morning," said the rebbe. "The whole month of Elul, we blow it after davening. I would hate for our class to be the only one that is silent."

At recess, nobody played punchball. Six boys sat on the steps instead, with Yehuda's blue notebook open on his knee.

"Facts first," said Yehuda. "When was it last seen?"

"Thursday," said Avrumi. "I saw Rabbi Feldman put it back after the shofar lesson. He wrapped it in the bag. I remember, because I drew it."

He flipped through his sketchbook and held up a page: the closet, the shelf, the blue bag, all in careful pencil.

"So it disappeared between Thursday and today," said Binyamin. "Who opened the closet since then?"

"The custodian," said Nosson. "On Friday, Mr. Gomez cleaned the whole room. I saw his cart in the hall when I went home early for Shabbos."
===
Tzvi Hirsch jumped up. "Then he took it! Let's go!"

"Sit down," said Yehuda, and everyone laughed, even Tzvi Hirsch, because they had all learned that lesson the year before, with the esrog.

"Let's think like Binyamin plays chess," said Avrumi. "List every place the shofar could be, then check them one by one."

They made a list. The music room, where the eighth graders practiced their tekiyos. The office, where lost things went. The shul downstairs, where the big shofar was kept. And the classroom itself.

They checked the music room first. A boy from eighth grade was practicing a long, wobbly tekiah on his own shofar, and his face was as red as a tomato. No blue bag. They checked the office. Mrs. Rivkin showed them a box of lost things: three mittens, a yarmulka, a broken calculator and a single sneaker. No blue bag. The shul was locked until Mincha.

"That leaves one place," said Binyamin quietly. "The classroom. Maybe it never left."

Dovid was looking at Avrumi's drawing very closely. "Look," he said. "In the picture, the closet has two shelves. The shofar is on the top one. But Mr. Gomez always puts the cleaning things on the top shelf when he dusts. Where do you think he would move the shofar, to keep it safe?"

Six boys ran back upstairs. Dovid opened the closet and got down on his knees. There, on the bottom shelf, behind a box of chalk and a stack of old siddurim, was a blue velvet bag.

Inside was the shofar, safe and whole, its smooth, curved horn shining in the light from the window.

When the rebbe heard the story, he smiled. "Mr. Gomez was not hiding it," he said. "He was protecting it. And you boys did not accuse anyone. You looked. That is the real tekiah."
--- words
velvet: soft, thick cloth with a smooth surface
disappeared: went where nobody could see it
custodian: the person who cleans and takes care of a building
protecting: keeping something safe
accuse: to say someone did something wrong
