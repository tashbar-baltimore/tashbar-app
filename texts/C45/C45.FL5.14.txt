title: The Eagle Has Landed (a play)
grade: 5
kind: play
topic: The Moon and Space
source: original
---
July 20, 1969. Inside Mission Control in Houston, Texas, dozens of engineers sit at long rows of screens. Far away, two astronauts are flying a small landing craft called the Eagle down toward the moon.

NARRATOR: Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin are only a few thousand feet above the moon. In Houston, one man is allowed to talk to them: Charlie Duke.

ALDRIN: Program alarm.

ARMSTRONG: It's a 1202. Give us a reading on the 1202 program alarm.

NARRATOR: Nobody in the room breathes. An alarm could mean danger. If it is serious, they must stop the landing and fly home.

ENGINEER: It's okay! The computer is just very busy. We can keep going!

DUKE: Eagle, we're go on that alarm.

NARRATOR: But now there is a new problem. Armstrong looks out the window. The computer is taking them toward a crater full of giant boulders.
===
NARRATOR: Armstrong takes over and flies the Eagle by hand, searching for a flat place to land. The fuel is running low.

DUKE: Sixty seconds.

NARRATOR: Sixty seconds of fuel are left. The Eagle keeps flying.

ALDRIN: Forward. Drifting right.

DUKE: Thirty seconds.

NARRATOR: In Houston, men grip the edges of their desks. Then, at last, a light turns on in the Eagle.

ALDRIN: Contact light!

NARRATOR: The landing legs have touched the ground.

ARMSTRONG: Houston, Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed.

DUKE: Roger, Tranquility. We copy you on the ground. You got a bunch of guys about to turn blue. We're breathing again. Thanks a lot!

NARRATOR: For the first time ever, people had landed on the moon. Hours later, Neil Armstrong climbed down the ladder and took the first step onto its dusty gray ground.
--- words
astronauts: people trained to travel into space
crater: a big round hole in the ground
boulders: very large rocks
drifting: moving slowly, pushed along
grip: to hold on to something very tightly
