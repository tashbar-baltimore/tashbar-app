My name is Avrumi Katz, and my topic today is how to read an old map. I chose this topic because, as some of you know, my friends and I have been looking at a very old map, and I learned that reading it is not the same as reading a new one.

First, look for the date. Many old maps have a date in a corner, sometimes written in small, curly letters. The date tells you which world the map is showing you. A street that is on a map from 1900 might not be there today. It might have a new name, or it might be gone completely.

Second, find the compass rose. That is the little star with the letters N, S, E and W. On most maps, north is at the top. But not on all of them! Some old mapmakers put east at the top, and if you do not check, you will walk in the wrong direction for a very long time.
===
Third, read the key. The key is a small box that tells you what each symbol means. A tiny anchor might mean a dock. A tiny square might mean a house. On one old map of Baltimore, a little flame meant a firehouse. Without the key, the symbols are just scribbles.

Fourth, check the scale. The scale tells you how far is far. One inch on the map might be one block, or it might be one whole mile. That is a big difference when you are walking!

And fifth, and this is the most important one: look for what does not belong. A smudge. A pencil mark. A word in different handwriting. Sometimes, the person who owned the map added something of his own. And sometimes, that is the most interesting part of all.

Thank you for listening. Are there any questions?
--- words
compass: a tool whose needle points north
symbol: a small picture that stands for something
scribbles: messy marks that do not mean anything
scale: the part of a map that shows how far things really are
smudge: a dirty, blurry mark
