Good afternoon, everyone. Let me start with a question. Why does soup go bad if you leave it out on the counter for a few days? Think about it for a moment.

For thousands of years, nobody really knew. Many people believed that spoiled food simply "turned bad" all by itself, that tiny living things just appeared out of nothing. It was a strange idea, but nobody could prove it wrong.

Then, in France, about one hundred and sixty years ago, a scientist named Louis Pasteur decided to find out. Pasteur was a careful man. He did not guess. He tested.

Here is one of his most famous experiments. He poured broth, a kind of soup, into glass flasks. Then he heated the necks of the flasks and bent them into long, curved shapes, like the neck of a swan. Air could still get in, but dust from the air got trapped in the curve. He boiled the broth to kill anything living in it, and he waited.
===
The broth in the swan-neck flasks stayed fresh. Days went by. Weeks went by. It did not spoil. But when he broke off the curved neck, so that dust could fall right in, the broth soon turned cloudy and bad.

So what was in the dust? Tiny living things, much too small to see without a microscope. Germs. Pasteur had shown that spoiling does not come from nothing. It comes from germs that land in the food and grow.

Pasteur did not stop there. Wine makers in France were losing whole barrels to spoiling. Pasteur found that if you warm a liquid gently, not boiling, just warm enough, for a short time, you kill most of the germs that spoil it. Later, the same idea was used for milk. Today we call it pasteurization, after him. Look at a carton of milk in your refrigerator tonight, and you will probably see that word.

So the next time your Ima tells you to put the soup back in the fridge, you can tell her: Louis Pasteur agrees.
--- words
broth: a thin, clear soup
flasks: glass bottles with narrow necks, used by scientists
experiments: careful tests to find out if an idea is true
pasteurization: warming a liquid to kill the germs that spoil it
refrigerator: a cold box in the kitchen that keeps food fresh
