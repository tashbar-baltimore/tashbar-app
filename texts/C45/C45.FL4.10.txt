title: A Cloud in a Jar

Good morning, boys. Today, I am going to make a cloud. Right here, on this desk. No, Tzvi Hirsch, it will not rain on your head. Probably.

Watch carefully. First, I pour a little hot water into this glass jar. Just a little, about two inches. See the steam? That is water turning into a gas called water vapor. You cannot see water vapor itself. The steam you see is tiny drops, already cooling.

Second, I put a metal plate on top of the jar. And on the plate, I put ice. Lots of ice. Now the air at the top of the jar is cold.

Now, here is the secret ingredient. I light a match, blow it out, and drop the smoke into the jar. Quickly! Cover it again.
===
Look! Do you see it? Inside the jar, a gray cloud is swirling around. That is a real cloud.

So what happened? Three things. Warm, wet air rose up from the hot water. At the top, the cold plate cooled it down. And when water vapor cools, it turns back into tiny drops of water. But the drops need something to hold on to. In the sky, that is dust. In our jar, it was the smoke.

Every cloud you have ever seen was made this way: warm, wet air rises, cools, and turns into billions of tiny drops, each one hugging a speck of dust.

Now, let me lift the plate. Watch the cloud escape! There it goes, floating up toward the ceiling.

Any questions? Yes, Dovid, you may try it at home. But only with your Abba, and only with a grown-up holding the matches. Understood? Good!
--- words
vapor: water as a gas, floating in the air
ingredient: one of the things you mix to make something
swirling: spinning around in circles
billions: thousands of millions; a huge number
speck: a very tiny piece
escape: to get out and get away
