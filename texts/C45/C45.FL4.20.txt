title: The Lion and the Mouse
grade: 4
kind: story
topic: Poems, Plays and Fables
source: adapted from Aesop
---
Long ago, in a hot, dry land, a great lion lay asleep in the shade of a tall tree. His mane was thick and golden. His paws were as big as dinner plates. Even in his sleep, he looked like a king.

A tiny gray mouse came running through the grass. She was in a hurry, and she was not looking where she was going. Before she knew it, she ran right up the lion's tail, across his back, and onto his nose.

The lion woke with a roar. In one quick move, he trapped the mouse under his giant paw.

"Who dares to wake the king?" he growled.

"Please, please, let me go!" squeaked the mouse. "I did not mean to wake you. I am so small. I would not even make you a snack. And if you let me go, maybe one day I can help you."

The lion laughed so hard that the leaves shook. "You? Help me? A tiny mouse help a lion?" But he was in a good mood, and he lifted his paw. "Go," he said. "Run home, little one."
===
The mouse ran off as fast as her little legs could carry her.

Many days later, the lion was walking through the forest when, suddenly, a net fell over him. Hunters had set a trap! The lion pulled. He rolled. He roared. But the more he fought, the tighter the ropes became.

Far away, the mouse heard the roar. She knew that voice. She ran and ran until she found the lion, tangled in the net.

"Be still," she said. "I will help you."

She began to chew on the thickest rope. Snip. Snap. One rope broke, then another, then another. At last, the lion pulled himself free.

The great lion looked down at the tiny mouse. "I laughed at you," he said quietly. "I was wrong. Thank you, my friend."

And from that day on, the lion never again laughed at someone just because he was small.

The lesson: nobody is too small to help, and a kindness is never wasted.
--- words
mane: the long, thick hair around a lion's head
trapped: caught so it could not get away
squeaked: made a short, high sound
tangled: twisted and caught in something
kindness: a good, gentle thing you do for someone
