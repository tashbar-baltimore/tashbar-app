Look at a map of Israel. The whole bottom part, shaped a bit like a triangle, is the Negev desert. It covers more than half of the country's land. The Negev gets very little rain, and its summers are long and hot. Long ago, many people thought almost nothing could grow there. Today, the Negev is full of farms, thanks to clever ideas that help farmers use every drop of water.

The most important idea is drip irrigation. With regular watering, a sprinkler sprays water all over a field. Much of it lands on bare ground or dries up in the hot air. Drip irrigation is different. Thin hoses with tiny holes run along the rows of plants. Water comes out drop by drop, right at the roots. Almost nothing is wasted, and each plant gets just what it needs.

Many years ago, an Israeli water engineer named Simcha Blass noticed something strange. In a row of trees, one tree grew much bigger than all the others. Next to it was a pipe with a tiny leak. Drip, drip, drip, the water fell right by the tree's roots. Years later, Blass and his son worked out a way to water whole fields like that leaky pipe. In the 1960s, a farming village in the Negev started a company to make the new drip hoses. Today, farmers all over the world use them.
===
Greenhouses help, too. A greenhouse is a building with walls and a roof made of clear plastic or glass. It lets the sunlight in, but it keeps out strong wind and blowing sand. Inside, water does not dry up as fast. Farmers can use shade nets and fans to keep the plants from getting too hot. Some farmers in the Negev also raise fish in big pools of water. Inside the greenhouses, long rows of peppers and tomatoes grow tall and healthy.

Farmers in the Negev also use water that has already been used once. Water from homes goes down the drain into big pipes. In Israel, much of that water is sent to big water-cleaning stations, called treatment plants, where it is cleaned. Then it flows through other pipes to the farms, where it waters fields and orchards. This water is not used for drinking, but it is fine for trees and crops. Israel recycles most of its used water, more than almost any other country in the world.
===
Many fruits and vegetables come from the Negev. Bright red, yellow, and orange peppers grow there. So do cherry tomatoes, melons, and eggplants. Tall date palms grow in the hot south, and their sweet, sticky dates are packed into boxes and sent far away. In the winter, when farms in Europe are cold, many Negev vegetables are shipped to stores there.

The Negev shows that a desert does not have to stay empty. With drip hoses, greenhouses, and recycled water, Israeli farmers grow food where it once seemed impossible. People from other dry countries now come to Israel to learn how they do it. The next time you crunch into a sweet red pepper, think about how it might have grown in the sunny, sandy Negev.
--- words
irrigation: bringing water to plants through pipes, hoses, or ditches
engineer: a person who plans and builds machines, pipes, roads, or bridges
leak: a small hole that lets water drip out
greenhouses: buildings with clear walls and roofs where plants grow
orchards: fields where many fruit trees are planted
recycles: uses something again instead of throwing it away
