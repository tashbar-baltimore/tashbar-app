title: The Glitter Test (a play)
grade: 5
kind: play
topic: Germs

---
The science fair in the yeshiva gym. Dovid stands behind a table with a bowl of water, a bar of soap, and a small jar of glitter. Mr Halpern walks up with a clipboard. Tzvi Hirsch and Binyamin watch.

MR HALPERN: Dovid, what is your project?

DOVID: How soap fights germs. But germs are too small to see, so I use glitter instead. Glitter sticks to your hands just like germs do.

TZVI HIRSCH: That is why my hands sparkle. He made me test it this morning.

DOVID: Watch. I put a little oil and glitter on Binyamin's hands. Binyamin, please wash with plain water.

BINYAMIN: I am washing. I am washing. It is not coming off.

DOVID: Exactly! Most germs stick in the oil on our skin. Water alone just slides over the oil.

MR HALPERN: And now the soap?
===
DOVID: Now the soap. A soap bit has two ends. One end loves water. The other end loves oil and grabs it. When you rub, the soap pulls the oil, and the germs stuck in it, right off your skin. Then the water carries it all away.

BINYAMIN: It is working! The glitter is going down the drain!

DOVID: But only if you rub long enough. About twenty seconds. That is why my Ima sings a song while my little brothers wash.

MR HALPERN: Very good. One question, Dovid. In your yeshiva, you also wash your hands before you eat bread. Is that for germs?

DOVID: No. That is netilas yadayim. It is a mitzvah, and we do it because it is a mitzvah, with a cup and a brachah, even when our hands are already clean. Soap is for germs. Netilas yadayim is for something much more important.

MR HALPERN: A very good answer. And a very good project.

TZVI HIRSCH: Does it get a prize?

MR HALPERN: It gets a prize. And you, Tzvi Hirsch, get soap.
--- words
project: a big piece of work that takes planning
glitter: tiny shiny pieces
sparkle: to shine with little flashes of light
mitzvah: a commandment from the Torah
