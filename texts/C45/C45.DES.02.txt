title: The Cactus Keeps a Secret
grade: 4
kind: article
topic: Deserts
source: original
---
Most garden plants would die in a desert. The ground is dry, and rain may not fall for months. But the cactus grows there, year after year. When there is more than one cactus, we call them cacti. How do cacti stay alive where other plants dry up? The cactus keeps a secret. Its whole body is made to catch water, store it, and hold on to it.

A cactus stores water inside its thick stem. The inside of the stem is soft and spongy. After a rain, the cactus soaks up as much water as it can and keeps it there. Then it lives on that water for a long time, even when no rain falls. The giant saguaro cactus has deep folds on its stem, like an accordion. The folds open wide as the stem fills with water. They pull closer together as the cactus uses the water up.

A cactus has spines instead of leaves. Big, flat leaves lose a lot of water into the air. Thin, sharp spines lose very little. The spines also keep hungry and thirsty animals from biting into the juicy stem. When many spines grow close together, they even make a bit of shade for the cactus. Since the cactus has no regular leaves, its green stem does the job of making food from sunlight.
===
Touch a smooth part of a cactus stem very carefully, between the spines. It feels slick and shiny. That shine comes from a thin coat of wax on the skin. Think of a bowl of leftover soup in the fridge. With plastic wrap on top, it stays wet for days. Without the wrap, it slowly dries out. The wax on a cactus works the same way. Many cacti also keep the tiny holes in their skin closed during the hot day. They open them at night, when the air is cooler, so less water escapes.

The roots of a cactus are shallow and wide. They do not dig deep into the ground. Instead, they spread out just under the surface, often far out to the sides. In the desert, rain does not soak in very deep. The wide roots are waiting right there to drink it up fast, before the hot sun dries it. After a storm, some cacti quickly grow extra tiny roots to catch even more water. Some people like to keep tiny cacti in pots on their windowsills.
===
Some cacti also grow fruit. The prickly pear cactus grows a sweet, juicy fruit, full of stored water and covered with tiny, sharp hairs. In Eretz Yisrael, this fruit is called a sabra. People peel it with care, and many enjoy it cold on a hot summer day.

Cacti come in many shapes and sizes. Some are tiny and round, about as big as a golf ball. Others are tall and thin, like green poles. The saguaro can grow taller than a house and live for many years. But big or small, every cactus uses the same tricks to stay alive.

A cactus may look quiet and still, but it is always working. Its stem stores water. Its spines and its wax keep that water from escaping into the air. Its roots grab every drop of rain that falls nearby. That is the secret that lets a cactus live in places where almost nothing else can grow.
--- words
spongy: soft and full of tiny spaces that can soak up water, like a sponge
accordion: a musical instrument with folds that open and close
spines: thin, sharp, pointy parts that grow on a cactus
wax: a smooth, slippery coat that water cannot get through
shallow: not deep; close to the top
surface: the top or outside part of something
