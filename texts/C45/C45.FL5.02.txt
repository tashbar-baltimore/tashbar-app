The second night of Chanukah, at Zeidy and Savta's house. Downstairs, Zeidy is polishing the menorah. Upstairs, Yehuda stands at the bottom of a narrow ladder with a flashlight.

NARRATOR: Every Chanukah since he was seven, Yehuda has been allowed to climb into Zeidy's attic and choose one box. Just one.

ZEIDY: Yehuda! You remember the rule?

YEHUDA: One box a year, Zeidy! Just one!

ZEIDY: And don't take all night. Savta's sfenj are almost ready.

NARRATOR: Yehuda climbs the ladder and pushes open the square door in the ceiling. The attic is cold and low, and it smells of dust and old paper.

YEHUDA: A trunk with leather straps. A hatbox. Seforim with gold letters, no, I took those last year. Letters from Morocco, no, the year before.
===
NARRATOR: He moves the flashlight slowly from box to box. Then the light lands on something he has never noticed before.

YEHUDA: What is that? Way in the back, behind the trunk, under the slanted roof?

NARRATOR: It is a flat wooden box, about as big as a large sefer. The wood is dark and smooth. The lock is orange with rust.

YEHUDA: Locked. Of course it's locked. Without a key, it's useless.

NARRATOR: He turns it over. On the bottom, held by a strip of old yellow tape, is a small iron key.

YEHUDA: A key! Taped underneath! Somebody wanted this box to be opened, but only by someone who looked very, very carefully.

ZEIDY: Yehuda! Did you choose?

YEHUDA: Yes, Zeidy! I chose!

NARRATOR: Yehuda peels off the tape, slowly, gently, so the old key will not break. He fits it into the lock. He turns it. The lock gives a tired little click.

YEHUDA: Here we go.

NARRATOR: And the lid begins to open.
--- words
polishing: rubbing something until it shines
narrow: thin, not wide
slanted: going up on one side, not flat
rust: the orange crust that grows on old wet iron
useless: not able to be used for anything
