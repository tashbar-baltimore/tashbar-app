title: Ship of the Desert

Good morning, boys, and welcome to the camel yard! My name is Mr. Weiss, and I take care of the camels here at the zoo. Come a little closer to the fence. That's it. Don't worry, Sheba is very gentle. She only spits when she is upset, and today she is in a good mood.

Look at her feet. They are wide and flat, like big soft pillows. When a camel walks on hot sand, her feet spread out, so she does not sink. That is why people call the camel the ship of the desert. She sails across the sand.

Now look at her eyes. Do you see her eyelashes? She has two rows of them, long and thick. In a sandstorm, they keep the sand out. She can even close her nose, so the sand cannot get in.
===
And her hump? Many people think the hump is full of water. It is not! The hump is full of fat. When there is no food in the desert, a camel can live on that fat for days, even weeks. When the fat is used up, the hump gets small and floppy.

So how does a camel live without water? She does not waste it. When she finally finds a well, she can drink more than twenty-five gallons in about ten minutes. That is as much as twenty-five big milk jugs!

In Eretz Yisrael, in the Negev desert, people have used camels for thousands of years. Our Avos traveled with camels. Camels carried spices, cloth and food from town to town.

Any questions? Yes, you, in the front. No, I'm sorry. You may not ride Sheba today. But you may pet her nose. Gently!
--- words
gentle: soft and kind, not rough
sink: to go down into something soft
sandstorm: strong wind that blows sand through the air
floppy: soft and hanging down
gallons: a way to measure water; a big milk jug holds one
spices: dried plant parts that give food a strong taste
