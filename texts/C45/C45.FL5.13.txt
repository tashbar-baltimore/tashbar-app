title: Galileo Looks Up
grade: 5
kind: speech
topic: The Moon and Space
source: original
---
Boys, look at this tube. Two pieces of glass, one at each end. It looks like a toy. In the year 1609, a tube like this changed the way people saw the sky.

That year, a teacher in Italy named Galileo Galilei heard about a new invention from Holland: a tube with glass lenses that made faraway things look close. He did not have one, so he built his own. Then he made a better one. Then a better one still, until things looked about twenty times closer.

Most people used these tubes to look at ships far out at sea. Galileo did something different. One clear night, he pointed his tube at the moon.

What did he see? Until then, many people believed the moon was perfectly smooth, like a polished ball. But through his tube, Galileo saw mountains, with long shadows behind them. He saw valleys and deep round holes, the ones we now call craters. The moon was not smooth at all. It was a world of rock, with hills and plains, like our earth.
===
Galileo kept looking. He pointed his tube at Jupiter, a bright planet. Next to it he saw three tiny points of light, and a few nights later, a fourth. Night after night, the little lights changed places. He realized they were moons, circling around Jupiter! He drew them carefully, night by night, in his notebook.

He pointed his tube at the Milky Way, the cloudy band across the night sky, and he saw that it was made of countless stars, too many to count, too faint to see with eyes alone.

Galileo wrote a small book about what he saw, and he called it The Starry Messenger. People all over Europe read it and could hardly believe it.

So, boys, here is my question for you. Galileo saw new things because he did not only look; he looked carefully, and he wrote down everything, night after night. What could you see, if you did the same?
--- words
lenses: curved pieces of glass that make things look bigger or clearer
polished: rubbed until smooth and shiny
plains: wide, flat lands
countless: too many to count
faint: very weak and hard to see
