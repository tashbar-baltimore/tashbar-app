title: When the Desert Floods

It may sound strange, but one of the biggest dangers in the desert is water. The desert is dry almost all the time. Then, every so often, a strong storm comes. Within minutes, a dry valley can fill with a roaring river. This is called a flash flood. It comes fast, like a flash of lightning, and it can be very dangerous.

Desert ground does not soak up water well. Months of hot sun bake the dirt until it is hard, like a clay pot. When rain falls on it, the water cannot sink in. It runs off the top instead, the way water runs off a driveway. In most deserts, there are also few plants with roots to slow the water down.

On a summer afternoon, the sky over the desert may be clear and blue. An hour later, tall, dark clouds can pile up high. Thunder booms, and heavy rain pours down all at once. Then, almost as fast as it came, the storm moves on, and the sun shines again.
===
All that water rushes into low, dry riverbeds. In America, these riverbeds are called washes. In Israel and other lands of the Middle East, they are called wadis. Most of the year, a wash or a wadi is just a dry path of sand and rocks. People hike along it, and animals walk through it. But when a storm comes, water from miles around pours into the same low path. It turns into a fast, muddy river that can sweep away rocks, branches, and even cars.

A flash flood can come even when the sky above you is sunny. The storm may be far away, over mountains many miles off. The water races down the wadi toward you. Hikers sometimes hear a loud roar before they see the water coming. That is why it is so important to watch the sky in every direction. After a good rain, many deserts also burst into bloom with bright wildflowers.
===
There are smart rules for staying safe. Before a desert trip, always check the weather report. Never camp or rest in a dry riverbed, even if it looks calm. If the water starts to rise, climb to high ground right away. Never walk or drive into moving water. Even water that looks shallow can knock a grown man off his feet or carry a car away. Weather experts in America have a short saying to help people remember: "Turn around, don't drown."

In Israel, people take flood warnings very seriously. Many groups go on trips to hike in the wadis of the Negev and the Judean Desert. Before each trip, the guides check for flood warnings. If rain is expected, the trip is changed or called off. Rabbi Feldman, who learned in Yerushalayim for many years, remembers a trip to the desert that was canceled because of a warning. He was disappointed, but he knew it was the right choice. The Torah teaches us to guard our health and safety with great care.

A flash flood is a surprise, but it does not have to be a danger. Hard, dry ground and sudden storms can turn a dry wadi into a rushing river in minutes. People who understand how flash floods work, and who follow the safety rules, can enjoy the beautiful desert and come home safe.
--- words
roaring: making a very loud, deep sound
bake: to make something hot and hard, like in an oven
riverbeds: the low ground where a river flows, or used to flow
wadis: dry riverbeds in the Middle East that fill with water after a storm
sweep: to carry away fast and hard
seriously: in a careful way, knowing that something matters a lot
canceled: called off, so that it did not happen
