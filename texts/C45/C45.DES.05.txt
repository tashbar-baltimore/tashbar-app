In the afternoon, desert sand can be hot enough to burn bare feet. Yet a few hours after the sun goes down, a person may need a warm coat. This surprises many people. Deserts are not only hot places. Many of them are hot by day and cold by night, and many desert animals wait for the cool night to come out.

Dry air is the first reason deserts cool down so fast. The air in most places holds water vapor, which is water in a form so tiny that you cannot see it. Water vapor holds in heat, like a warm blanket. Desert air has very little water vapor in it. So when the sun sets, there is not much to keep the day's heat near the ground, and the heat escapes quickly.

Think about a cloudy night at home in Baltimore. The clouds hang over the city like a cozy quilt. Heat from the ground rises up, and much of it is held in under the clouds. Over the desert, the sky is usually clear, with no clouds at all. The heat rises up and up, all the way into space.
===
Because days are so hot, many desert animals sleep in the daytime and come out at night. Animals that are busy at night are called nocturnal. During the day, they rest in burrows under the ground, where it is cooler. When the sun goes down, they come out to hunt and look for food. The cool night helps them save water, because they do not lose as much water from their bodies in the dark.

The fennec fox is one of the best-known night animals of the desert. It lives in the Sahara, a huge desert in North Africa. It is the smallest fox in the world, about the size of a small cat. Its ears are huge for such a small animal. Those big ears let heat leave its body, and they help it hear insects moving under the sand. Thick fur on the bottoms of its feet protects it from hot sand.
===
A jerboa looks like a tiny kangaroo with a mouse's face. It hops on two long back legs and can leap far in a single jump. Its long tail has a tuft of fur at the end, and the tail helps it keep its balance. During the day, it sleeps in a burrow and plugs the door with sand to keep out the heat. At night it comes out to gather seeds and bits of plants. It gets most of the water it needs from its food, so it hardly ever needs to drink.

Scorpions are night hunters, too. In the day, a scorpion hides under a rock or in a crack in the ground. At night it creeps out to catch insects and spiders. It can feel tiny shakes in the ground when its prey moves nearby. Then it grabs the prey with its two strong pincers. Strangely, scorpions glow a bright blue-green color under a special purple lamp called a black light. A scorpion can sting, so people should never touch one.

So the next time you think of the desert, do not picture only blazing sun. Picture a cold, clear night with thousands of stars. Picture a fennec fox listening, a jerboa hopping, and a scorpion creeping over the sand. Dry air and clear skies make desert nights cold, and when the sun goes down, the desert wakes up.
--- words
vapor: water in a form so tiny that it floats in the air and cannot be seen
escapes: gets out and goes away
nocturnal: awake and busy at night, asleep in the day
burrows: holes or tunnels in the ground where animals live
tuft: a small bunch of hair or fur growing close together
prey: an animal that is hunted by another animal for food
pincers: the two strong claws a scorpion uses to grab things
