Rabbi Feldman, parents, and boys, good evening.

Tonight our class finished a whole perek of Mishnayos, and Rabbi Feldman asked each of us to say a few words about our chavrusa. My chavrusa is Tzvi Hirsch Gold. Anyone who knows us is already smiling, because everyone knows we are opposites.

I am slow. Tzvi Hirsch is fast. I like to read a mishna three times before I say a word. Tzvi Hirsch likes to say the answer before I finish reading the question. When we started learning together in Elul, I was sure it would never work.

The first week, we argued about everything. He wanted to skip ahead. I wanted to go back. One day, we spent the whole time arguing about one word, and we never even got to the next line. I went home that night and told my Zeidy, "I need a new chavrusa."

My Zeidy listened. Then he said something I will never forget. He said, "Binyamin, a chavrusa who agrees with everything you say is a mirror. A chavrusa who argues with you is a window. Through a window, you can see something new."
===
So I went back. And slowly, things changed.

Tzvi Hirsch taught me that sometimes the first idea is the right one, and you do not have to wait forever to say it. I taught him, I hope, that sometimes it is worth going back and reading the words one more time. When he got stuck, I helped him. When I got stuck, he jumped in with an idea, and sometimes, I have to admit, it was a very good idea.

Tonight, when we finished the last mishna, Tzvi Hirsch did something he has never done before. He waited. He let me finish reading. Then he said, very quietly, "Again?" And we read it one more time, together.

So, Tzvi Hirsch, thank you. Thank you for being my window.

And to everyone here: may we all find chavrusas who argue with us, and who make us better.
--- words
perek: a chapter
opposites: two people or things that are as different as can be
argued: said that we did not agree, again and again
mirror: a glass that shows you yourself
