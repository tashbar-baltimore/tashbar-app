Dear Pinny,

You asked what I am building this month, so here is the whole story, from the beginning. Get comfortable. It has a problem in the middle, like every good story.

The garden shed behind our house has no electricity. Every time Abba goes out at night to find a rake or a ladder, he has to bring a flashlight, and the flashlight always seems to be in the other room. So I decided to build a windmill that would make electricity for a light in the shed.

Here is how it works. When the wind turns the blades, they spin a little motor. A motor that is spun by something else does the opposite of its normal job: instead of using electricity, it makes electricity. That electricity charges a battery, and the battery runs a small light.

I made the blades from a plastic pipe that Abba helped me cut. I put everything on a pole next to the shed. Then I waited for the wind.
===
And then came the problem. The wind blew, the blades turned, and the light glowed so weakly that you could barely see it. I was so disappointed that I didn't even want to eat supper. Well, I ate supper. But slowly.

Abba, who is an engineer, asked me two questions. "Where is the wind strongest?" and "How much wind are your blades catching?" I thought about it all evening, even on the walk home from Maariv. Near the ground, the fence and the shed block the wind. Higher up, it blows freely. And my blades were so short that most of the wind just slipped past them.

So we made two changes. We put the windmill on a taller pole, above the roof of the shed. And I made the blades longer, so they could catch more wind.

On the first windy night after that, I ran outside, opened the shed door, and switched on the light. It shone! Abba came out, found his ladder in two seconds, and gave me a hug that lifted me off the ground.

Come visit on Chanukah, and I will show you. Bring a warm coat. Windmills like cold, windy nights.

Your cousin,
Dovid
--- words
disappointed: sad because something did not turn out the way you hoped
engineer: a person who plans and builds machines, bridges or buildings
freely: without anything stopping it
