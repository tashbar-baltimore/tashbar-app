Slowly, Nosson brought his hands out from behind his back.

In his right hand was the magnifying glass.

"Rabbi Feldman asked me to bring this to the office," he said. "Before recess. But the office was locked, so I came back." He looked at the empty box. "Where is the esrog?"

"That," said Tzvi Hirsch, folding his arms, "is exactly what we want to know."

Nosson's cheeks turned pink. He put the magnifying glass down on the rebbe's desk very carefully, the way you put down something that is not yours, and walked to his seat without another word.

Just then Mrs. Rivkin from the office hurried in. She had heard the noise all the way down the hall. When she saw the empty box, she pressed her lips together.

"Rabbi Feldman had to step out for a few minutes," she said. "I'm sure there is a simple answer. Everyone sit down and read quietly until he comes back."

"But the esrog—" began Dovid.

"Read," said Mrs. Rivkin, in the voice that ended all conversations.

The boys sat. Twenty-two books opened. Almost nobody read.
===
Yehuda sat with his book open to page 40 and his blue notebook hidden inside it. Next to him, Avrumi had already started a new drawing: a map of the classroom, with a little box for the rebbe's desk, little squares for the desks, the door, the window and the hooks. He drew a tiny hat with an X through it.

Binyamin leaned across the aisle. "In chess," he whispered, "when you don't know what your opponent is planning, you list every piece that can move. Then you check them one by one."

"So we list everyone who could have taken it," whispered Yehuda.

"Everyone who was near the room at recess," said Avrumi, still drawing.

Tzvi Hirsch, who sat behind them, leaned forward. "That's easy. One person was in the room. One." He pointed his pencil at the back of Nosson's head.

"Shh," said Binyamin. "That is one piece. We said every piece."

So during "quiet reading," on a page of Yehuda's notebook, the Glen Avenue Crew made a list. They whispered and wrote and crossed out, and when they were done, it looked like this:
===
1. Rabbi Feldman. He was guarding it. His hat is gone.
2. Nosson Abergel. Stayed inside at recess.
3. Mrs. Rivkin. Works in the office downstairs.
4. Mr. Gomez, the custodian. Was fixing the heater in the hall.
5. The fifth-grade boys. Their room is next door.
6. Somebody from outside? The front door is locked during school.
7. The wind?? The window is open.

"The wind?" Tzvi Hirsch snorted. "Wind can't steal an esrog."

"Look at the window," said Avrumi.

They looked. The tall window next to the rebbe's desk was open about a hand's width. The wind was still blowing hard, and every few seconds a gust pushed in and lifted the corners of the papers on the bulletin board. On the floor under the radiator lay a few worksheets that had blown off the desk. A few wet orange leaves had blown in too, and lay by the rebbe's desk.

"An esrog is too heavy to blow away," said Dovid. Dovid knew about these things. In his basement he had a fan, a scale and a box of things he had tested. "But papers aren't."

Yehuda wrote window open, papers on the floor in the notebook and drew a star next to it. He was not sure why the papers mattered. He only knew that a good detective writes down everything.
===
Then Tzvi Hirsch said, "Look at Nosson's sleeve."

They all looked, as casually as five boys can look at the same thing at the same time.

On the cuff of Nosson's dark blue sweater, there were little white wisps, soft and fuzzy, like bits of cotton.

Yehuda knew those wisps. Every year before Sukkos, his Zeidy Benaim brought home an esrog wrapped in exactly that kind of fluff. Zeidy called it flax. It kept the esrog safe, like a pillow.

"That's flax," Yehuda breathed. "The stuff you wrap an esrog in."

"You see? That's suspicious," whispered Tzvi Hirsch. His face was red. "He took it. He wrapped it up and hid it somewhere. Case closed."

For a moment, nobody said anything. It did look bad. Nosson had been alone with the esrog. He had come in with his hands behind his back. And now there was esrog fluff on his sleeve.

Yehuda felt his stomach tighten. He did not like the feeling. He looked at Nosson, who was reading his book, or pretending to. Nosson's shoulders were hunched up near his ears. He turned a page, then turned it back.

"Wait," said Yehuda slowly. "The esrog in the box wasn't wrapped in flax. It was wrapped in a white cloth. Remember? I saw it."
===
Avrumi flipped back two pages in his math book and pointed to his drawing from that morning. There was the esrog, with its tiny dots, lying on a smooth cloth. No fluff anywhere.

"So where did the flax come from?" asked Binyamin.

Nobody knew.

Tzvi Hirsch frowned. "Doesn't matter. He still could have taken it."

"Could have," said Binyamin, "is not the same as did."

Just then, the door at the front of the room swung open, and the whole class jumped.

But it was only Mr. Gomez, the custodian, with his toolbox. "Sorry, boys," he said. "Heater's fixed. Your rebbe asked me to close that window." He shut it with a bang, nodded and left.

Yehuda stared at the closed door.

Your rebbe asked me. When? Where? If Mr. Gomez had talked to Rabbi Feldman, then Mr. Gomez knew where Rabbi Feldman was.

Yehuda tore a page out of his notebook, wrote ASK MR. GOMEZ WHERE HE SAW THE REBBE in big letters, and folded it into a tiny square.

He was passing it to Avrumi when a hand came down on the desk between them.

It was Nosson Abergel's hand.

"I know what you are writing about me," Nosson said quietly. "And you are wrong."
--- words
casually: in a relaxed way, so nobody notices anything special
flax: soft fluff used to wrap and protect an esrog
custodian: the man who takes care of and fixes the school building
hunched: pulled up and bent forward, like when you feel bad
suspicious: seeming like someone did something wrong
opponent: the person playing against you in a game
