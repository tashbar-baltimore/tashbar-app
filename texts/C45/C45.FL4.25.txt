Dear Rabbi Feldman,

You asked us to write a letter about something we learned outside of school. So I am writing about the best Sunday of my whole year: the day I helped my uncle in his bee yard.

My uncle Gershon keeps bees on his farm near Westminster. On Sunday he gave me a white suit, gloves, and a hat with a net over my face. I looked like an astronaut. My little brother laughed so hard he fell off the porch.

First, my uncle used a smoker. It puffs cool, gentle smoke near the hive. The smoke makes the bees calm, so they do not get upset when you open their home. Then he lifted out a wooden frame. It was covered with bees, thousands of them, crawling over the honeycomb. My heart was beating very fast. But the bees did not care about me at all. They just kept working.
===
Here is what surprised me most. My uncle said that bees are not kosher, but honey is kosher. I asked how that could be. He said Chazal explain that the bees do not make the honey out of their own bodies. They only gather nectar from flowers and store it. So the honey is kosher, even though the bee is not. I think that is amazing.

My uncle also told me why farmers love bees. When a bee goes from flower to flower, she carries pollen, and that helps the apples, the cherries and the almonds grow. Without bees, my uncle said, many fruits would never grow at all.

At the end of the day, we cut the wax caps off a frame and let the honey drip into a big jar. I tasted it right from the spoon. It was the best honey in the world.

I am bringing a jar to class on Monday. There is enough for everyone.

Your talmid,
Avrumi Katz
--- words
astronaut: a person trained to travel into space
smoker: a can that puffs smoke to calm bees
gather: to collect things into one place
pollen: yellow powder from flowers that helps plants make fruit
