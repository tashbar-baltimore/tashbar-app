Meknes, Morocco
The fifth night of Chanukah

My dear son Yosef,

Your letter came on Tuesday, with a stamp of a tall ship on it, and I read it out loud three times: once to your father, once to your sisters, and once more to myself, very slowly, so that I would not miss a single word.

You write that Baltimore is cold, that the snow comes up to your knees, and that the shul on your street has radiators that knock and bang all night like a family of woodpeckers. Here, the oranges are ripe in the courtyard, the sky is blue, and your little sister Simcha asks every morning, "Did Yosef write today?"

We lit the candles tonight in the window, the way we always do. Your father sang Maoz Tzur in his deep voice, and when he reached the last line, he stopped and looked at your empty chair. Then he smiled and said, "In Baltimore, too, there is a window, and in that window, too, there are candles." So, my son, we are not so far apart after all.
===
I made sfenj, of course. Too many, of course. The neighbors ate the rest, and Madame Toledano, who never praises anyone's cooking, said that mine were "not bad," which, from her, is a very great compliment.

Please, Yosef, eat properly. Wear the wool scarf. Learn every night, even when you are tired, even when the work at the shop is hard. And find a good shul, with good people, where they will know your name.

I am sending a small package with this letter. Inside, wrapped in a cloth and packed in a tin, is a jar of my salted lemons, so that your food in Baltimore will taste, at least a little, like home. Open it carefully. Share it with your friends at the shop, and tell them it comes from Meknes.

With all my love, and with a prayer every night that we will see each other again soon,

Your mother
--- words
courtyard: an open space with walls around it, next to a house
radiators: metal heaters that warm a room with hot water
compliment: something nice you say about someone
properly: in the right way
package: a box or bundle sent by mail
