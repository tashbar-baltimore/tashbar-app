title: Twelve Seconds (a play)
grade: 4
kind: play
topic: The First Airplanes
source: original
---
Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. The morning of December 17, 1903. A cold wind blows across the sand. Two brothers from Dayton, Ohio, stand next to a strange machine made of wood, wire and cloth. A man from the lifesaving station nearby holds a camera.

NARRATOR: For four years, Wilbur and Orville Wright have tried to build a machine that can fly. They fixed bicycles to pay for it. They crashed gliders, again and again. Today, they will try once more.

WILBUR: The wind is strong, Orville. Twenty miles an hour, at least.

ORVILLE: Good. A strong wind helps us lift. Is the engine ready?

WILBUR: Ready. It is your turn to fly. I had my turn three days ago, and I crashed it.

ORVILLE: Then I will try not to crash it.

JOHN DANIELS: Mister Wright, what do I do with this camera?

ORVILLE: When the machine leaves the ground, squeeze the bulb. Just once. Do not miss it!
===
NARRATOR: Orville lies down on the lower wing. The engine coughs, then roars. The propellers spin. The machine slides along a wooden rail, faster and faster.

WILBUR: Go, go, go!

NARRATOR: And then, it lifts. One foot. Five feet. Ten feet into the air.

JOHN DANIELS: It's flying! It's really flying!

NARRATOR: The machine wobbles up and down in the wind. Twelve seconds later, it bumps back onto the sand, one hundred and twenty feet from where it started.

WILBUR: Orville! Twelve seconds! You flew for twelve seconds!

ORVILLE: It was not very far. A boy could throw a ball farther.

WILBUR: But it was the first time. Nobody, ever, has done that before.

JOHN DANIELS: Did I squeeze the bulb at the right time? I was so excited, I am not sure.

NARRATOR: He did. His picture of that first flight is one of the most famous photographs in the world.
--- words
lifesaving: helping people in danger, especially at sea
gliders: light flying machines with no engine
propellers: spinning blades that push a plane through the air
wobbles: moves unsteadily from side to side
famous: known by very many people
