id: C45.FL5.18
title: The Firemen's Train
grade: 5
kind: poem
topic: The Great Baltimore Fire
source: original
---
The telegraph clicks in Washington town:
"Baltimore's burning! The city goes down!"
The firemen run, and the horses are fed,
the engines are hitched to the flatcars ahead.

Clackety-clack on the iron rail,
over the river and up the vale,
forty long miles through the winter gray,
faster, faster, and on its way!

The firemen stand with their collars high,
watching the smoke climb into the sky.
Black at the start, then orange and red:
"Look at the city," the captain said.

Clackety-clack, and the brakes give a squeal.
Into the station of brick and steel.
Down come the engines, out come the hose,
into the streets where the hot wind blows.

They reach for the hydrant, they turn and they twist:
the hose will not fit, and the water is missed.
They wrap it in canvas, they bind it up tight,
and fight with the others all through the night.

And when the great fire stopped at the river's bend,
Baltimore called them "brother" and "friend."
--- words
telegraph: a machine that sent messages along wires
flatcars: train cars with flat tops for carrying big things
hydrant: a big pipe on the street where firemen get water
canvas: a thick, strong cloth
