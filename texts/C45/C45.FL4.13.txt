Good morning, campers! My name is Reb Akiva, and I lead the hikes here at Camp Pine Lake. Tomorrow morning, we climb Ridge Trail. Before anyone puts on a backpack, we have the most important talk of the summer. So please put down the canteens, Tzvi Hirsch, and listen.

A trail is a gift. It takes you up mountains, past waterfalls, and into the quiet woods, where you can hear birds you never knew existed. But a trail is also a teacher, and it does not give second chances. So here are my four rules of the trail.

Rule one: stay together. Every group has a leader at the front and a counselor at the back. Nobody walks ahead of the leader. Nobody falls behind the counselor. If you cannot see the person in front of you, stop and call out.

Rule two: stay on the trail. Look for the painted marks on the trees. On this mountain, our trail is marked with blue paint. If you have not seen a blue mark for a long time, you have left the trail. Go back to the last mark you saw.
===
Rule three: carry water, and drink it. Not when you are thirsty. Before you are thirsty! On a hot day, a hiker can lose a lot of water without even noticing.

Rule four, and listen carefully, because this one is the most important: if you are lost, stop. Do not run. Do not wander. Sit down next to a big tree, blow your whistle three times, and wait. We will find you. A boy who stays in one place is found quickly. A boy who keeps walking is very hard to find.

Now, who can tell me the four rules? Yes, Avrumi. Stay together, stay on the trail, drink water, and if you are lost, stop and blow the whistle. Excellent! Everyone, take your whistle, and let's go see the mountain.
--- words
canteens: bottles for carrying water on a hike
existed: were real; were there
counselor: a grown-up or older boy who takes care of a group at camp
wander: to walk around with no plan
whistle: a small tool you blow into to make a loud, high sound
