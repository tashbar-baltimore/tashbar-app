"Dovid!" Yehuda called down the hall. "Come back! Bring your ruler!"

Dovid Lerner never needed to be asked twice when there was a problem that needed a tool. He came running back into the classroom with a ruler in one hand and his backpack swinging from the other. Behind him came Avrumi, Binyamin, Tzvi Hirsch and, a little more slowly, Nosson.

"Behind the radiator," said Yehuda, pointing. "The yellow corner. See it?"

Dovid lay flat on the floor and squinted. "It's stuck between the fins. If I just push it, it will fall down further." He opened his backpack, took out a roll of tape, and wrapped a piece around the end of the ruler with the sticky side out. "Fishing pole," he explained.

The others knelt around him and held their breath. Dovid slid the ruler slowly between the fins. Once, the tape caught the wrong paper, a worksheet about fractions, and Tzvi Hirsch groaned. On the second try, the ruler came out with a small, square, yellow paper stuck to the end.

Dovid peeled it off and handed it to Yehuda.

It was a sticky note, a little bent and dusty. On it, in Rabbi Feldman's neat, slanted handwriting, were the words:

Back soon. The esrog is with me. R.F.

For a moment, nobody said anything. Then Binyamin let out a long breath.
===
"So it's true," he said. "Everything he told us. He did write a note. The wind took it."

"Of course it's true," said Tzvi Hirsch. "He's the rebbe."

"We didn't know it was true," said Yehuda slowly. "Not for sure. Now we have proof." He took out his blue notebook and stuck the yellow note inside the cover, very carefully, so it would not get more bent. "I'm going to give it back to him tomorrow."

They sat on the floor by the radiator, because nobody felt like getting up yet.

"Let's see what we got right," said Avrumi. He unfolded his map of the classroom, now covered with arrows, X's and tiny drawings. "The hat was gone, but the coat was here. We said that meant he was somewhere close. Right."

"The leaves on the stairs went up," said Dovid. "We said he went upstairs. Right."

"The cloth was folded neatly," said Yehuda. "We said whoever took it was careful. Right."

"And we said I stole it," said Nosson, in a small voice. "Wrong."

Tzvi Hirsch turned red to the tips of his ears. "I said that. Not everybody. Me."

"You said sorry already," said Nosson. "It's okay."

"It's not okay that I said it," said Tzvi Hirsch. "But it's okay that you forgave me." He thought for a moment. "Is that how it works?"
===
"I think that's exactly how it works," said Binyamin.

Mr. Gomez's cart rattled somewhere down the hall. Outside, the wind had finally calmed down, and the late afternoon sun made long stripes across the floor.

"There's one thing we still don't know," said Yehuda. "The spot. Is the esrog really ruined?"

"The Rav will say tomorrow," said Dovid. "We can't do anything about it."

Nosson was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "Maybe we can. My grandfather grew up in Meknes, in Morocco, where his family traded esrogim. My father learned all about them from him. Every year, people bring him their esrogim to look at before Sukkos." He hesitated. "You could come to my house tonight. Avrumi, you could bring your drawing."

Yehuda's face lit up. "My Savta is from Morocco too! From Meknes!"

"So is my grandfather," said Nosson, and the two boys stared at each other as if they had just found a missing piece of a puzzle.

That evening, six boys crowded around the kitchen table at the Abergel house. Nosson's mother put out a plate of warm almond cookies and a pot of mint tea. Nosson's father, a tall man with a short gray beard and reading glasses pushed up on his forehead, smoothed out Avrumi's drawing of the esrog on the table.
===
He looked at it for a long time. He pulled his glasses down onto his nose. He looked again.

"Avrumi," he said at last, "you drew this before recess? Before the rebbe picked it up?"

"Yes," said Avrumi. "In the morning. I copied every bump."

Mr. Abergel tapped one tiny dot on the drawing, near the top, right by the stem.

"Then tell me," he said, "why is the spot already here?"
--- words
squinted: looked with eyes half closed to see better
slanted: leaning to one side
forgave: stopped being upset at someone who said sorry
traded: bought and sold
hesitated: stopped for a moment before doing something
proof: something that shows for sure that it is true
