Tuesday night, Camp Pine Lake. Written in my camp notebook, by Tzvi Hirsch Gold, Bunk Daled.

Reb Akiva says that when something big happens to you, you should write it down while you still remember it. So I am writing it down. My hand is still a little shaky, so if this is messy, that is why.

This is my side of the story. I can only tell what I saw and heard and felt. There is a lot I do not know yet. I will get to that.

The first thing I remember is the dog. She came flying out of the bushes like a brown rocket and ran straight across our stone arrow. She knocked me right over. I was lying flat on my back on the rock, and she was standing on my chest, licking my whole face, my nose, my ears, everything. Her tongue was warm and wet and smelled like dog food. I have never been so happy to be licked in my life. I was laughing and crying at the same time, and I did not even care that Avrumi could see me crying.
===
Then a man came out of the trees. He was tall, with a gray beard and a green ranger hat, and a radio on his shoulder. He was breathing hard, like he had been running uphill for a long time. He probably had been.

"Scout, sit," he said. The dog sat, right on my stomach. That is when I found out her name was Scout.

The man knelt down next to us. "Are you Tzvi Hirsch and Avrumi?" he asked. We both nodded. I could not talk. "I'm Ranger Morales," he said. "Is either of you hurt?"

I said no. Avrumi said no. The ranger looked at our hands, which were all scraped from the rocks, and at our wet sneakers, and he asked us a lot of questions. Did we have water? When did we last eat? Did anything hurt when we stood up? Then he pressed the button on his radio, and I will never forget what he said, because I heard it with my own ears.

"Found. Both boys. Both okay."

From the radio came a crackle and then a lot of voices at once, all cheering. I think one of them was Reb Akiva. It sounded like his voice, only higher.
===
Ranger Morales gave us each a bottle of water and a granola bar. I ate mine in about three bites. He looked at our stone arrow for a long time.

"You boys made this?" he said.

"Avrumi planned it," I said. "I carried most of the rocks."

"And the orange flag on the big rock? That was smart." He shook his head slowly. "And you stayed put. Most people, when they get lost, they keep walking and walking and get more lost. You two did exactly the right thing."

I wanted to tell him that it was not me. I wanted to tell him that I was the one who ran after a cap, and that I almost ran down the mountain in the dark, twice. But my throat felt tight, so I just looked at Avrumi. Avrumi was looking at his sneakers, and his ears were red. That is what Avrumi's ears do when somebody says something nice about him.

Then we walked down the mountain. It took a long time, more than an hour. Ranger Morales went first, and Scout went back and forth between us like she was making sure nobody got lost again. I stayed right behind the ranger the whole way. I did not run ahead even one time. I did not even want to.
===
When we came out of the woods at the bottom, there was a road, and a ranger truck, and a camp van, and a lot of people. Reb Akiva was there. He did not say anything at first. He just grabbed both of us and hugged us so tight that my face got squished against his whistle. When he let go, his eyes were wet. I had never seen a counselor cry before.

Rabbi Weiss, the camp director, was there too. He handed me his phone. "Your mother," he said.

I said, "Hello?"

My mother said my name. Just my name, Tzvi Hirsch, and then she did not say anything for a while, and I could hear that she was crying, and behind her I could hear my father saying, "Is it him? Is he okay?" and the twins making their noises.

I said, "I'm okay, Ima. I'm really okay. I'm sorry."

She said, "You have nothing to be sorry for. You're safe. That's all that matters." But she said it in a voice that told me we would talk about the cap later.
===
Now here is what I learned. I am going to write it down so I don't forget it.

I learned that when you are lost, you stop. I always thought being brave meant doing something fast. Running into things. Jumping first. But the bravest thing I did on that mountain was sit still. It was the hardest thing too.

I learned that Avrumi Katz is the smartest person I know, and that he is braver than he thinks. He came down the slope after me when he did not have to. He said "Stop" when I did not want to hear it.

I learned that a squashed rugelach can taste like home.

And I learned that I can be scared and still do the right thing. Both at the same time.

There is one thing I still do not get, though. When the ranger found us, he came from the west side of the ridge. He went straight to our hollow, like he already knew where to look. But nobody saw us leave the trail. Nobody saw which way we went.

So how did they know to look on the west side of the ridge?
