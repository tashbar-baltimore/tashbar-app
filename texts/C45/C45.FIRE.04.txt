title: From Ashes to a New City: How Baltimore Rebuilt
grade: 5

topic: The Great Baltimore Fire of 1904
source: original
---
On Tuesday morning, February 9, 1904, the people of Baltimore walked into a city they did not know. Where busy streets had been, there were piles of bricks, twisted iron and smoking ashes. More than 1,500 buildings were gone, across about 140 acres. Banks, offices, warehouses and shops had burned. Thousands of people had no place to work.

Many people asked a hard question. Could the city ever come back?

The answer came fast. Within days, business owners were renting rooms in other parts of the city so they could open again. Bankers worked out of houses and hotels. Shopkeepers sold from carts and from the front rooms of their homes. Baltimore was hurt, but it was not finished.
===
Cities all over the country offered to send money to help. The mayor, Robert McLane, thanked them, but he said that Baltimore would rebuild with its own strength. Some people thought he was too proud. Others were glad. Either way, the city now had a big job to do.

The first job was cleaning up. Workers with wagons and horses hauled away mountains of broken bricks and burned wood. Walls that were about to fall had to be pulled down before anyone could build.

The second job was planning. The city made a group of leaders called the Burnt District Commission. Their job was to decide how the new downtown should look. They did not want to build the old city again, with all of its problems. They wanted a better one.
===
The old downtown had many narrow, crooked streets. Wagons got stuck in them, and fire engines could hardly get through. So the planners made some streets wider and straighter. The old docks along the harbor were crowded and falling apart. So the city rebuilt them, and the new docks were bigger and stronger.

There was another problem, one that people could smell. Baltimore had almost no sewers. Dirty water from homes ran into open ditches and into the harbor, which made the city smell bad and made people sick. Since the streets were already torn up, it was a good time to fix this. Soon after the fire, Baltimore began building a modern sewer system under its streets.

The new buildings were different too. Many were built with stronger walls, metal frames and better fire doors. New rules said how buildings had to be built, so that one fire could not jump so easily from roof to roof.
===
The work went faster than anyone expected. Builders worked from early morning until dark. Trains brought in bricks, stone and steel from other cities. Little by little, tall new buildings rose where the ashes had been.

In September 1906, only two and a half years after the fire, Baltimore held a week of parades and celebrations. People came from all over to see the new downtown. Bands played, and the streets were filled with flags. The city wanted the whole country to know that Baltimore was back.

The fire was a terrible loss. But it taught the city an important lesson. When something is destroyed, you do not have to build back the same thing. You can learn from what went wrong and build something better. That is what the people of Baltimore did, one brick at a time.
--- words
hauled: pulled or carried something heavy
commission: a group of people chosen to do one special job
crooked: not straight; bending this way and that
docks: places at the edge of the water where ships load and unload
sewers: pipes under the ground that carry dirty water away
frames: the strong inside parts that hold a building up
