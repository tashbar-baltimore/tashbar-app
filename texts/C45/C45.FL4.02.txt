The classroom, right after recess. The silver box is open on the rebbe's desk. It is empty. Six boys crowd around it.

DOVID: It's empty! The esrog is gone!

TZVI HIRSCH: Somebody took it. Somebody stole the rebbe's esrog!

YEHUDA: Wait. Don't touch anything.

TZVI HIRSCH: Why not?

YEHUDA: Because that is what detectives say. And because there might be clues.

BINYAMIN: Let's stay calm. In chess, when you don't know your opponent's plan, you list every piece that can move. Then you check them, one by one.

AVRUMI: So we list everyone who was near the room at recess.

TZVI HIRSCH: That's easy. Only one boy stayed inside. One!

DOVID: You mean Nosson? The new boy?

TZVI HIRSCH: Who else? Yehuda saw him walking toward the stairs with his hands behind his back!
===
YEHUDA: I saw him walking. I didn't see him take anything.

TZVI HIRSCH: Hands behind his back, Yehuda. What was he hiding?

AVRUMI: Maybe a sandwich. Maybe a sefer. Maybe nothing.

BINYAMIN: A good player never moves before he looks at the whole board.

YEHUDA: Look at the cloth. It is folded in half, neatly. Would a thief stop to fold the cloth?

DOVID: Hmm. I would not. I would grab it and run.

TZVI HIRSCH: Okay, okay. But who else is on the list?

AVRUMI: The rebbe's hat is gone. His coat is still on the hook. So the rebbe went out, but not far.

YEHUDA: Then he is still in the building. And so, maybe, is the esrog.

The door creaks open. Every head turns.

ALL: Shh!
--- words
opponent: the person playing against you in a game
clues: small facts that help you solve a mystery
neatly: in a careful, tidy way
thief: a person who steals
creaks: makes a long, squeaky sound
