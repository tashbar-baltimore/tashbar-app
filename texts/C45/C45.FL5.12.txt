On board the research ship Dawn Explorer
The Pacific Ocean, somewhere very wet

Dear Dovid,

Your Abba says you have been asking every day whether your uncle has reached the bottom of the ocean yet. The answer is: not me, but our robot has, and I watched every minute of it on a screen, with my coffee getting cold.

Here is where we are. We are floating above the Mariana Trench, the deepest place in all the oceans. If you dropped Mount Everest, the tallest mountain in the world, into the deepest part of the trench, its top would still be more than a mile under water. That is how deep it is: almost seven miles straight down.

Down there, it is completely dark. No sunlight reaches that far, not even a little. The water is just above freezing. And the pressure, the weight of all that water pressing down, is enormous: more than a thousand times the push of the air on your skin. It would crush a submarine like a soda can, unless the submarine were built very, very strong.
===
So you are probably wondering: is anything alive down there? Yes! Our robot's lights found tiny shrimp-like creatures, pale and almost see-through, swimming in the dark as if it were the most normal place in the world. The scientists on board cheered as loud as a shul on Simchas Torah.

Now, the question your Ima will ask: what do I eat? I brought two boxes of kosher food and my own small oven, which is used only for my food. Everything is double-wrapped, and the ship's cook calls my corner of the kitchen "the kosher island." On Friday, I lit candles in a jar on the deck, with the whole Pacific for a dining room. It was the most beautiful Shabbos table I have ever had, even without Bubby's kugel.

Learn hard, help your Ima, and keep building your machines. When I come home, I will show you the video of the robot. You can tell me how you would build a better one.

Love,
Uncle Reuven
--- words
pressure: the force of something pressing hard
enormous: very, very big
crush: to press so hard that it breaks
creatures: living things, like animals
