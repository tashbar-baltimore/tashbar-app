On Monday morning, Rabbi Feldman walked into the classroom with a silver box in his hands. He held it the way you hold a full cup of hot tea. Slowly. Carefully. With both hands.

"Boys," he said, "please clear your desks."

Twenty-two desks went quiet. Even Tzvi Hirsch Gold closed his mouth.

The rebbe set the box down and lifted the lid. Inside, on a soft white cloth, lay an esrog. It was bright yellow, like sunshine on a Shabbos afternoon. A tiny brown stem stood up at the top, straight as a soldier.

"Whoa!" whispered Dovid Lerner. He leaned so far forward that his chair tipped, and Avrumi had to grab it before it fell.

"Is it real?" asked Binyamin.

"It is very real," said Rabbi Feldman. "A friend of mine picked it from his own tree in Eretz Yisrael. Today we will learn how to check an esrog. Is it hadar? Is it beautiful enough for the mitzvah?"
===
He passed around a magnifying glass. One by one, the boys came up to look. Nobody was allowed to touch.

"Look with your eyes," said the rebbe, "not with your fingers!"

Yehuda Benaim looked for a long time. He noticed that the esrog was wider at the bottom, like a pear. He noticed a tiny scratch on the silver lid. He noticed that the cloth smelled sweet and sharp, like lemons. He wrote all of it in the small blue notebook he kept in his pocket. Yehuda always wrote things down. You never knew which small thing would turn out to matter.

"Nu, Yehuda," said Rabbi Feldman with a smile, "are you going to buy it?"

The whole class laughed. Yehuda's ears turned hot, and he hurried back to his seat.

Then the bell rang for recess. The boys grabbed their jackets and ran for the door. Not one of them guessed that when they came back, the silver box would be empty.
--- words
carefully: slowly, paying close attention so nothing goes wrong
hadar: beautiful enough for the mitzvah
magnifying: making something look bigger than it really is
noticed: saw something and paid attention to it
scratch: a thin mark cut into something
