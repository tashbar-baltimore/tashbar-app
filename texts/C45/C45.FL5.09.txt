Dear Nosson,

Rabbi Feldman says that at the end of the year we should each write a letter to someone who helped us grow. Most boys are writing to their parents or to a rebbe. I am writing to you, and I think you will be surprised.

Do you remember your first week in our class, back in fourth grade, when the esrog went missing? I remember it every time I see a silver box. You walked in with your hands behind your back, and I pointed at you, in front of everybody, and I said you took it.

You did not take it. You did not even argue. You just turned pink and went to your seat. Later, when I told you I was wrong, you shrugged and said, "You didn't know." Then I said sorry. That was all. You never brought it up again. Not once.
===
Here is what I did not tell you then. That shrug changed me.

Before that week, I always said the first thing that came into my head. If something went wrong, I looked for someone to blame, fast, because fast felt strong. After that week, every time I wanted to point at somebody, I saw your pink face, and I stopped. I counted. I asked a question first. My Abba noticed. He says I am getting "almost patient," which, from him, is a big compliment.

And I think, maybe, I changed you a little too. When you came, you hardly talked. Now you argue with me about everything, from punchball rules to the best kind of rugelach. You are wrong about the rugelach, by the way. Chocolate is better than cinnamon. Everybody knows that.

So thank you, Nosson. Thank you for being quiet when I was loud, so that I could hear myself.

Your friend,
Tzvi Hirsch

P.S. Come to the bakery on Friday. I will prove it about the chocolate.
--- words
argue: to tell someone you do not agree, and why
shrugged: lifted the shoulders to show "it doesn't matter"
blame: to say that someone did something wrong
patient: calm while waiting or when things go wrong
compliment: something nice you say about someone
