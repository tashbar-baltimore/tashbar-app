title: Built for the Desert: The Camel

---
The desert is one of the hardest places on earth to live. The sun beats down all day long. Water is very hard to find. Strong winds blow sand into everything. Yet one large animal can walk across the desert for days without trouble. That animal is the camel, and almost every part of its body is built for desert life.

The camel's hump is not a water tank. Many people think it is, but that is a mistake. The hump is really a big lump of fat. When food is hard to find, the camel's body uses the fat for energy. As the fat gets used up, the hump grows smaller. It can even flop over to one side! When the camel eats well again, the hump fills back up. Keeping its fat in one spot also helps the rest of the camel's body stay cooler.

There are two kinds of camels. Camels with one hump are called dromedaries. They live mostly in the Middle East and North Africa. Camels with two humps are called Bactrian camels. They live in rocky deserts in Asia, where the winters are cold and can even bring snow.
===
Walk across soft sand, and your feet sink with every step. Each step takes extra work. A camel's foot has two toes and a wide, thick pad underneath. When the camel steps down, the pad spreads out flat, like a snowshoe. The pad is tough and leathery, so hot sand does not burn it. A camel can walk on sand all day and hardly sink at all.

When the wind blows hard in the desert, a sandstorm can fill the air with stinging grains of sand. A camel has two rows of long eyelashes that catch the sand before it reaches its eyes. It also has a thin, clear third eyelid. This eyelid wipes sand away like a windshield wiper, and the camel can see through it. The camel's nostrils are narrow slits that it can squeeze shut. Even its ears have hair inside to keep the sand out.
===
A camel can go a long time without drinking. In hot weather, a camel can travel for about a week or more with no water at all. In cooler weather, when it eats juicy green plants, it may go for many weeks. Its body wastes very little water. A camel does not even start to sweat until its body gets very hot. When a thirsty camel finally finds water, it can drink a huge amount in just a few minutes. Camels can also be very noisy, and they grunt and groan when people load bags onto their backs.

People have used camels for thousands of years. Camels carried heavy loads across the desert in long lines called caravans. Their soft hair was made into warm cloth and blankets. In the Torah, Eliezer, the servant of Avraham Avinu, traveled with ten camels to find a wife for Yitzchak. Rivka showed her kindness by giving water to every one of those thirsty camels.

From its fatty hump to its wide feet, the camel is a perfect fit for the desert. Every part of its body helps it save water, stay cool, or keep out the sand. It is no wonder that people call the camel "the ship of the desert." Like a ship crossing the sea, a camel can carry people and their things across miles of empty sand.
--- words
flop: to fall over in a loose, floppy way
dromedaries: camels that have only one hump
leathery: tough and thick, like leather
sandstorm: a strong wind that blows lots of sand through the air
nostrils: the two openings in the nose that air goes in and out of
caravans: long lines of camels and people traveling together across the desert
