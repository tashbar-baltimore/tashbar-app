id: C45.FL4.14
title: Campfire Night
grade: 4
kind: poem
topic: Camp and the Outdoors
source: original
---
The sun goes down behind the hill.
The lake is dark, the air is still.
We gather wood, both thick and thin,
and build our fire. Then we begin.

A crackle, then a pop, a spark!
The flames leap up against the dark.
They dance in orange, red and gold,
and chase away the evening cold.

Reb Shaya tells a story, slow,
of camp, and rain, so long ago,
when all the tents blew down one night
and everyone was fine by light.

We sing a niggun, soft and low,
that rises with the embers' glow.
It floats above the sleepy trees,
and drifts across the lake with ease.

At last the fire is ash and gray.
We walk to bunks along the way,
our flashlights wobbling, one by one,
and sleep, and dream of morning sun.
--- words
crackle: a quick, sharp, snapping sound
embers: small pieces of wood still glowing after a fire
drifts: floats slowly, carried by air or water
wobbling: moving unsteadily from side to side
