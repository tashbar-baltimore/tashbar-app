Friday morning, in the Gold family's kosher bakery on the corner. Trays of challah cool on the racks. Tzvi Hirsch stands by an empty tray, with his father and his friends.

ABBA: Boys, I baked two dozen chocolate rugelach for Mrs. Stern's Shabbos order. I put them right here, on this tray, to cool. Now the tray is empty!

TZVI HIRSCH: Empty? All twenty-four?

ABBA: All twenty-four. Not even a crumb.

DOVID: Did anybody come into the back room?

ABBA: Only the delivery man, at seven o'clock. And you boys, at eight.

TZVI HIRSCH: Abba! You don't think we ate them?

ABBA: I don't think anything yet. I am asking questions.

YEHUDA: Good. Questions come before answers. May I look at the tray?
===
ABBA: Go ahead, Yehuda.

YEHUDA: There are no crumbs at all. When a person eats rugelach, there are always crumbs. Always!

DOVID: It's true. My little brother leaves crumbs on the ceiling.

YEHUDA: So maybe nobody ate them here. Maybe somebody carried them away, on another tray.

AVRUMI: Mr. Gold, what did the delivery man bring this morning?

ABBA: Flour, sugar, and boxes for Shabbos orders. He took away the boxes that were packed and ready.

TZVI HIRSCH: Boxes that were packed! Abba, which boxes were packed?

ABBA: Let me see the list. Oh. Oh, no.

ALL: What?

ABBA: At six o'clock, I packed Mrs. Stern's order myself. To save time, I moved the warm rugelach from this tray into her box. And then I forgot!

TZVI HIRSCH: So the rugelach are on their way to Mrs. Stern?

ABBA: Right on time! Boys, I owe each of you an apology, and a hot chocolate rugelach. A fresh one!
--- words
dozen: a group of twelve
delivery: bringing things to a place
crumb: a tiny piece of bread or cake
packed: put into a box, ready to go
apology: saying you are sorry
