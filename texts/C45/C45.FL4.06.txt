Good afternoon. This is Eli Shapiro with the weather, live from the Negev desert in the south of Eretz Yisrael.

Here, the sky is blue. The sun is shining. There is not one drop of rain. But listen carefully, because there is danger on the way.

Far to the north, in the mountains, a big storm is pouring rain. And in the desert, rain does not stay where it falls. The ground here is hard and dry, almost like stone. It cannot soak up the water. So the water rushes downhill, into the dry riverbeds. In Eretz Yisrael, people call a dry riverbed a wadi.

In just a few minutes, a quiet wadi can turn into a roaring river! This is called a flash flood. A flash flood can be taller than a grown man. It can carry rocks, trees, and even cars.
===
So here is what you must do today.

Number one: stay out of every wadi, even if the sky above you is blue.

Number two: if you hear a roar, like a train, climb to high ground right away. Do not wait. Do not go back for your bag.

Number three: never, ever try to cross moving water. Not on foot, and not in a car.

The good news? After the flood comes something wonderful. In a few days, the desert will bloom. Tiny seeds have been waiting in the sand for months, even years. Now they will sprout, and the brown hills will turn green, yellow and purple.

That's the weather. Stay safe, stay dry, and have a good afternoon!
--- words
danger: something that could hurt you
soak: to take in water, like a sponge
riverbeds: the bottoms of rivers, where water flows
roaring: making a deep, loud noise
bloom: to open into flowers
sprout: to start growing out of the ground
