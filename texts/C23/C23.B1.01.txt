It was a rainy Sunday on Shabbos Street. Naftali Rosen sat in his Zeidy's workshop. The workshop was full of clocks. Big clocks, small clocks, and clocks with no hands at all. Tick, tick, tick, they all said together.

"Zeidy, may I help you fix a clock?" Naftali asked.

"You may dust the old drawer," said Zeidy. "Nobody has opened it in years."

Naftali pulled the drawer. It was stuck. He pulled again, harder. The drawer flew open, and dust puffed up like a little cloud. Naftali sneezed.
===
At the bottom of the drawer was a piece of yellow paper. It was folded four times. Naftali opened it slowly.

It was a map! There was a street with tiny houses. There was a river with a bridge. There was a big tree with a red X under it. But the paper was torn right down the middle. Half of the map was missing.

"Zeidy, look!" Naftali called. "A map with an X!"

Zeidy put on his glasses. He looked at the map for a long time. Then he smiled a small smile.

"Where did the other half go?" Naftali asked.

"That," said Zeidy, "is a very good question."
===
That afternoon, the Shabbos Street Club met in the Rosens' garage. The club had four boys. Naftali was the builder. Avi Benhamou loved maps and jokes. Chaim Weiss read every book in the shul library. Dovid Katz was the youngest, and he was always hungry.

Naftali put the map on the table. The boys leaned in close.

"An X means treasure," said Dovid. "Maybe it is a treasure of cookies."

Avi laughed. Then he looked closer. "This river is our river," he said. "And this bridge is the old bridge by the park."

Chaim tapped the torn edge. "Then the other half shows where the tree is," he said. "We have to find it."

The boys looked at each other. A real map. A real X. A real mystery.

"The Shabbos Street Club," said Naftali, "has a new case."
