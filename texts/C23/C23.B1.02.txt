On Monday after school, the club made a plan. "First, we ask," said Chaim. "Somebody tore this map. Somebody might have the other half."

They walked to the corner grocery. Mr. Benhamou, Avi's father, was stacking apples.

"Abba, have you ever seen this map?" Avi asked.

Mr. Benhamou looked at it. He shook his head. "No," he said. "But this paper is very old. Ask somebody who has lived on Shabbos Street a long time."

Dovid looked at the cookies by the counter. Mr. Benhamou smiled and gave each boy one.
===
Next they went to the shul. Rabbi Feld was in the library, putting seforim back on the shelf.

"Rebbi, do you know who drew this map?" Naftali asked.

Rabbi Feld held the map up to the window. "Look here," he said. In the corner, in tiny letters, were two letters: Y. R.

"Y. R.," said Chaim slowly. "Who has the letters Y. R.?"

Naftali felt his heart jump. "My Zeidy's name is Yaakov Rosen!" he said.
===
The boys ran all the way back to the workshop. Zeidy was fixing a tall clock.

"Zeidy, did you draw this map?" Naftali asked.

Zeidy put down his tools. "I did," he said. "A long time ago, when I was eight years old, just like you. My best friend and I drew it together. Then we each kept half."

"Who was your best friend?" asked Avi.

Zeidy looked out the window at the rain. "His name was Shmuel," he said quietly. "His family moved away. I have not seen him in sixty years."

The boys were quiet. Then Chaim said, "If Shmuel kept his half, maybe he still has it."

Zeidy smiled his small smile again. He reached into his pocket and took out a tiny brass key.

"Then you will need this," he said.
