On a clear night, Shmuel Kaplan stood on his back porch and counted stars. He gave up at two hundred. "There are too many," he told his Zeidy. Zeidy smiled. "You missed the most important one," he said. "You saw it this morning. It is the sun."

The sun is a star. It is the same kind of thing as the tiny points of light Shmuel saw at night. A star is a giant ball of very hot gas that gives off its own light and heat. The sun looks much bigger and brighter than the other stars for one simple reason: it is much, much closer to us.

How close? The sun is about 93 million miles away. That sounds far, and it is. A jet plane flying without stopping would need about twenty years to get there. But the next closest star is about 270,000 times farther away than the sun! Its light takes more than four years to reach us. That is why the other stars look like tiny dots.
===
The sun is enormous. About 109 Earths could line up side by side across its face, and you could fit more than one million Earths inside it. Even so, the sun is only a medium-sized star. Some stars are many times bigger. They look small to us only because they are so far away.

The sun is also very hot. Its surface is about 10,000 degrees Fahrenheit. That is hotter than any oven or fire on Earth. The center of the sun is hotter still: millions of degrees. Deep inside, the sun makes its energy, and that energy flows out into space as light and heat.

A little of that light and heat reaches Earth. It takes about eight minutes to travel here. That means the sunlight on your face left the sun eight minutes ago!

From far away, the sun looks calm and smooth. Up close, it is wild. Its surface bubbles and boils like a pot of soup. Giant loops of glowing gas leap up from it, some of them much taller than the whole Earth. Darker, cooler patches called sunspots come and go across its face.

Never look straight at the sun, not even for a moment. Its light is so strong that it can hurt your eyes for good. Scientists who study the sun use special filters, and so should you.
===
Without the sun, there would be no life on Earth. Its light lets plants grow, and plants feed animals and people. Its heat keeps our oceans from freezing solid. The sun's heat also moves the air and water around the world, which gives us wind, clouds and rain.

The sun seems to rise in the east and set in the west. But the sun is not really moving across our sky. The Earth spins like a top, and it turns all the way around once every day. When your side of the Earth turns toward the sun, it is day. When your side turns away from the sun, it is night.

In the Torah, the sun is called the great light that rules the day. Every morning, our tefillos thank Hashem for making the lights of the sky. The next time the sun comes up, think about Shmuel's lesson. The brightest star in the sky is the one that lights up our day.
