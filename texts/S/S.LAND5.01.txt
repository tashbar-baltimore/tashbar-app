Nosson Abergel's family flew to Eretz Yisroel for Pesach. Nosson sat by the window. For hours, all he could see below was blue. "Abba," he asked, "is the whole world water?" His Abba laughed. "Almost," he said. "About seven out of every ten parts of the earth's surface are covered by ocean."

Geographers name five oceans. From biggest to smallest, they are the Pacific, the Atlantic, the Indian, the Southern and the Arctic. But all five are really connected. Water flows from one into the next, so scientists often call them one world ocean. The Pacific alone is so wide that every piece of land on earth could fit inside it, with room to spare.

Ocean water is salty. One big bottle of sea water, a liter, holds about two big spoonfuls of salt. Rivers slowly carry tiny bits of salt out of rocks and into the sea, and the salt stays there. People cannot drink ocean water. It is far too salty, and drinking it would make a thirsty person even more thirsty.
===
The ocean is not only wide. It is also very deep. Near the shore, the water is shallow enough to wade in. Farther out, the bottom drops away. Coral reefs grow in warm, shallow water, where sunlight shines all the way down. Deeper still, whales dive to hunt for food in water that is almost dark.

The deepest place in the whole ocean is called the Mariana Trench, in the Pacific. It is about 11 kilometers deep. Mount Everest, the tallest mountain on land, is about 9 kilometers tall. If you dropped Everest into the trench, its top would still be about 2 kilometers under the water!

Scientists split the ocean into layers. The top layer is the sunlit zone. Here sunlight is strong, seaweed grows, and most sea animals live. Below it is the twilight zone, where only a little light gets through. Deeper than that is the midnight zone. No sunlight ever reaches it. The water is icy cold, and it presses down with enormous weight.
===
How can anything live in the midnight zone? Its animals have special ways to handle the dark. Some fish make their own light, glowing like tiny lanterns. The anglerfish has a glowing lure on its head that draws smaller fish close to its mouth. Other animals have huge eyes to catch the faintest light, or no eyes at all, finding food by touch and smell instead.

People have explored only a small part of the deep ocean. In many ways it is harder to reach than the moon, because the deep water is dark, freezing and heavy. Special submarines with thick walls can dive down, but only a few people have ever visited the very bottom of the Mariana Trench.

When Nosson's plane finally landed, he looked back at the blue Mediterranean Sea. In Tehillim, Dovid HaMelech sings about the great, wide sea and the creatures in it: "How many are Your works!" Nosson thought about the glowing fish far below, in water no sunlight has ever touched. He understood a little better why.
--- words
surface: the outside or top layer of something
geographers: scientists who study the earth and its lands and waters
connected: joined together
trench: a long, deep, narrow cut in the ground or the sea floor
zone: an area or layer with its own special features
lure: something that tricks an animal into coming closer
faintest: weakest and hardest to see
