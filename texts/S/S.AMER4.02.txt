Mr. Halpern rolled a long strip of paper across three desks. "This is a timeline," he said, and wrote the first date: 1492.

Long ago, people in Europe wanted spices and silk from Asia. The trip over land was long and dangerous, so sailors set out by sea. These explorers traveled to unknown places to find new routes and to map the world.

In 1492, a sailor named Christopher Columbus sailed west from Spain with three small ships. He hoped to reach Asia. Instead, after about ten weeks at sea, he reached islands near two great continents that Europeans did not know about: the Americas. Millions of people already lived there, in many nations with their own languages and ways of life.

That same year was a sad one for the Jews. The king and queen of Spain ordered every Jew to leave the country or give up the Torah. Many thousands left their homes. Yehuda Benaim's Zeidy says that some of their own family's grandparents were among them, and found a new home in Morocco.
===
Other explorers followed. In 1519, Ferdinand Magellan set out from Spain with five ships. Magellan himself died on the way, but in 1522 one of his ships came home. Its sailors were the first to sail all the way around the world.

Soon England sent people to live in America. In 1607 they built Jamestown, in Virginia, the first English town in America that lasted. Over the years, England started thirteen colonies along the Atlantic coast. A colony is land ruled by a faraway country. The northern colonies were called New England.

One colony is very close to home. In 1634, settlers sent by the Calvert family began Maryland. The head of the family was an English nobleman with the title Baron Baltimore. Our city, Baltimore, is named after him.

In 1776, the thirteen colonies declared their independence from England. They became the first thirteen states. That is why each stripe on the American flag stands for one of them.
===
In 1620, a group called the Pilgrims crossed the ocean from England on a small ship, the Mayflower. They wanted the freedom to worship in their own way. In December they came ashore at Plymouth, in what is now Massachusetts. The first winter was terrible. About half of them died from cold and sickness.

In the spring, a man named Squanto came to help. He was from the Patuxet people, who had lived on that land for ages. Years before, an English ship captain had kidnapped him and carried him across the ocean. He learned English there, and at last he found his way home. But while he was gone, a terrible sickness had swept through his village. Now Squanto showed the Pilgrims how to plant corn, with a small fish in each mound of soil to feed it. He also helped them talk with Massasoit, the leader of the Wampanoag people.

In the fall of 1621, the Pilgrims gathered a good harvest. They held a feast of thanks that lasted three days, and Massasoit came with about ninety of his men.

Many years later, in 1863, President Abraham Lincoln made Thanksgiving a holiday for the whole nation. Tzvi Hirsch Gold looked at the finished timeline. "We thank Hashem every day," he said. "It is nice when a whole country says thank you too."
