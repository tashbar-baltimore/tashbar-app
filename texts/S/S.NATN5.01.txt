On the Fourth of July, Yehuda Benaim waved a small paper flag on his front porch. The next morning he went straight to Mr. Halpern. "How did America start?" he asked. "Who decided that we would be our own country?"

Mr. Halpern smiled. "That is a long road," he said. "Let us walk it one step at a time."

In the 1760s, the thirteen colonies still belonged to Britain. Britain had just fought a long and costly war in North America, and it needed money. So Parliament, the group in London that made Britain's laws, decided that the colonists should pay new taxes. A tax is money that people must pay to the government.

In 1765, Parliament passed the Stamp Act. It put a tax on almost every piece of printed paper: newspapers, legal papers, even playing cards. The colonists were angry. Not one of them sat in Parliament. Representation means having someone who speaks for you where the laws are made, so that you have a voice in the government. The colonists had no such voice. Their answer became famous: "No taxation without representation!"
===
Britain took back the Stamp Act, but soon it tried other taxes, among them a tax on tea. On a cold December night in 1773, a group of colonists in Boston climbed onto three ships and dumped hundreds of chests of British tea into the harbor. People called it the Boston Tea Party. Many colonists also refused to buy British goods at all.

The king was furious. Britain closed Boston's harbor and sent more soldiers. In April 1775, British soldiers and colonists fought at Lexington and Concord, two towns in Massachusetts. The night before, a rider named Paul Revere had galloped through the dark to warn that British soldiers were coming. The fighting had begun.

Leaders from the colonies met in a group called the Continental Congress. They needed a general to lead their army. They chose George Washington of Virginia. He had been a soldier before, he stayed calm in danger, and his men trusted him.

"So now they were at war," said Dovid Lerner. "But they were still part of Britain?"

"Not for long," said Mr. Halpern.
===
In 1776, Congress asked Thomas Jefferson, a young leader from Virginia, to write a statement to the whole world. He wrote most of the Declaration of Independence. It explained why the colonies were breaking away from Britain. It said that all men are created equal, with rights that no king may take away: life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness. Liberty means freedom.

On July 4, 1776, Congress approved the Declaration in Philadelphia. The thirteen colonies now called themselves the United States of America. That is why we celebrate July 4 as Independence Day.

Words on paper did not end the war. Washington's army was often cold and hungry, and it lost many battles. But it did not give up, and families at home gave what they could to help. In 1781, the Americans and their French friends won a great battle at Yorktown, in Virginia. In 1783, Britain signed a treaty and agreed that America was free.

Yehuda wrote the dates in his notebook: 1765, 1773, 1775, 1776 and 1783. "Five dates," he said, "and a whole new country."
