For the last story of the unit, Benny Okafor-Levine wanted to write about something the empires could not destroy. "Our Gemara," he said, holding up his own. "Where did it come from?"

Mr. Gross told him to ask their rebbe, and two days later Benny came back with a full notebook.

At Har Sinai, Moshe Rabbeinu received the Torah in two parts. The Written Torah is the Chumash, written down word for word. The Oral Torah, Torah She'baal Peh, explains how to keep the mitzvos of the Written Torah. For example, the Chumash commands us to put on tefillin, but it is the Oral Torah that teaches what tefillin look like and how they are made. For many generations, the Oral Torah was not written in a book. It was passed down by mouth, from teacher to student. Each teacher said the words carefully, and each student reviewed them again and again until he knew them by heart.

Then came the Churban. While the Romans surrounded Yerushalayim, the great sage Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai was carried out of the city. He asked the Roman leader for one thing: Yavneh and its sages. After the Churban, Torah learning went on at Yavneh, and a small town became a center of Torah. The sages there kept teaching, and the chain of the Oral Torah was not broken, even after the Beis Hamikdash was gone.
===
But hard times went on. Jews were scattered to many lands, and many great teachers were killed. The sages feared that, with students so far from their teachers, the Oral Torah could be forgotten.

So Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi, the leader of the Jewish people in Eretz Yisroel, gathered the teachings of the sages and wrote down the Mishnah. He was so great that he is often called simply Rebbi. The Mishnah is short and clear, and it is arranged in six sections, called the six sedarim, each about one area of halacha. One seder teaches the laws of Shabbos and Yom Tov, and another begins with brachos and the laws of farming. The sages whose teachings fill the Mishnah are called the Tannaim.

Now the Oral Torah was guarded in writing. But a short, clear Mishnah still needs a teacher to explain it. Why does it say this and not that? How does one Mishnah fit with another? Those questions were the next great job.

Benny underlined one line in his notebook: "Rebbi wrote the Mishnah so that the Torah would never be lost, even in galus."
===
In the generations after Rebbi, the sages who learned the Mishnah deeply and explained it are called the Amoraim. Many of the greatest Amoraim lived in Bavel, where Jews had lived ever since the first galus. Great yeshivos grew there, in cities such as Sura and Pumbedisa, and thousands of students came to learn. For hundreds of years, the yeshivos of Bavel were the center of Torah for the whole Jewish world.

The Amoraim asked questions on every Mishnah. They answered them, compared one Mishnah with another and argued each side until they found the truth. Their discussions are the Gemara. The Gemara of Bavel, the Talmud Bavli, was completed in Bavel. The great Amoraim Rav Ashi and Ravina gathered it together. The sages of Eretz Yisroel had their own Gemara too, the Talmud Yerushalmi. But the Talmud Bavli became the Gemara that boys learn in yeshivos all over the world today. When a boy opens a Gemara, he joins the questions and answers of the Amoraim, as if he were sitting in their yeshiva.

Benny finished his story with a drawing: a boy in Baltimore, a boy in Yerushalayim and a boy in London, each opening the very same daf. Eli Mizrahi read it over his shoulder. "Asking questions and arguing each side," he said with a grin. "So that is where I get it from."

Akiva Feldman laughed, then tapped the drawing. "The empires are gone," he said, "and the Gemara is still on our tables. That is our headline: Torah lasts through every galus."
--- words
generations: the people born and living at about the same time
sage: a very wise Torah teacher
scattered: spread out to many far places
gathered: brought together into one place
sections: parts of a whole
compared: looked at two things to see how they are alike and different
discussions: talks in which people share and test their ideas
