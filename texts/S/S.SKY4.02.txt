Mr. Halpern drew a big yellow circle on the board. "This is the sun," he said. "Now we will take a tour of its family." He drew eight smaller circles in a long line. "These are the planets. Each one travels around the sun on its own path, called an orbit."

A planet is a large, round world that moves around a star. A planet does not make its own light. We can see some planets at night only because sunlight bounces off them. Earth is one of the eight planets that travel around our sun. Together, the sun and everything that moves around it are called the solar system. Moons are part of the solar system too. A moon travels around a planet, the way our moon travels around the Earth. Mercury and Venus have no moons at all, but Saturn has well over one hundred.

To remember the planets in order from the sun, Mr. Halpern taught a sentence: "My Very Excited Mother Just Served Us Noodles." The first letter of each word stands for a planet: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
===
The first four planets are the inner planets. They are small and made of rock and metal. Mercury is the closest to the sun and the smallest planet. A year on Mercury, one trip around the sun, takes only 88 days. Venus is covered with thick clouds that trap heat, so it is the hottest planet of all, even hotter than Mercury. Earth is the only planet we know of with liquid water on its surface and living things. Mars is called the red planet because its rocks and dust are full of rust. It has the tallest volcano in the solar system.

Between Mars and Jupiter is a wide belt of rocks called the asteroid belt. Some of these asteroids are as small as a pebble. A few are as wide as a whole country. Scientists keep careful track of them with telescopes. Past this belt are the outer planets.
===
The four outer planets are giants. They are made mostly of gas and have no solid ground to stand on. Jupiter is the biggest planet. More than one thousand Earths could fit inside it. A storm on Jupiter called the Great Red Spot is wider than our whole planet. Saturn is famous for its bright rings, which are made of billions of pieces of ice and rock. Uranus spins on its side, as if it had been knocked over. Neptune is the farthest planet from the sun. It is dark, cold and very windy. Its winds blow faster than any wind on Earth, faster even than the speed of sound. A year on Neptune lasts about 165 Earth years!

The farther a planet is from the sun, the longer its orbit, and the colder it is. "So which planet has the best spot?" asked Mr. Halpern. The boys knew the answer. Earth is not too hot and not too cold. It is just the right distance from the sun for water, air and life.
--- words
orbit: the path a planet or moon takes around something bigger
solar: having to do with the sun
inner: on the inside, closer to the middle
asteroid: a rock that travels around the sun, too small to be a planet
giants: things that are much bigger than others of their kind
liquid: wet and able to flow, like water or juice
