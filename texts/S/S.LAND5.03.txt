title: Water Under Our Feet

When rain falls on a field, where does it go? Some of it runs off into streams. Some evaporates back into the air. But much of it soaks down into the ground. It trickles through the soil and into the tiny spaces between grains of sand and cracks in the rock below. Water stored underground like this is called groundwater.

Groundwater is not one big underground lake. It is more like a sponge. The water fills the spaces in the sand, gravel and rock, the same way water fills the holes in a wet sponge. Loose sand and gravel let water soak through fast. Tightly packed clay lets it through only very slowly.

Dig a hole deep enough, and you will reach ground that is completely soaked with water. The top of this soaked ground is called the water table. Above the water table, the ground is damp. Below it, every space is full.
===
A well is a deep hole that reaches below the water table. Water seeps from the soaked ground into the well, and people pull it up with a bucket or a pump. A well must go below the water table to give water. A hole that stops above it will come up dry.

The water table can move up and down. After a rainy season, it rises. In a long dry spell, it can sink lower. Then a shallow well may run dry, while a deeper well still gives water. In some places, groundwater flows out of the side of a hill all by itself. That is a spring.

Wells were precious in the time of the Avos. In Bereishis 26, Yitzchak Avinu dug again the wells that his father Avraham had dug, which the Plishtim had stopped up with dirt. His servants dug new wells too. The shepherds of Gerar argued over the first two, called Esek and Sitnah. The third well no one fought over. Yitzchak named it Rechovos, saying, "Now Hashem has made room for us."
===
Groundwater can be spoiled. Oil, paint, garbage and strong cleaning chemicals that soak into the ground can flow down into the water that wells draw up. Once groundwater is dirty, it can take many years to become clean again.

The Torah teaches us to guard the world we live in. When an army is fighting a city, the Torah says not to cut down its fruit trees (Devarim 20:19). Chazal learn from this the mitzvah of bal tashchis: we may not waste or destroy anything for no reason. That means food, clothing, toys and water too.

Chazal also tell that when Hashem created Adam, He showed him all the trees of Gan Eden. Hashem said: see how beautiful My works are. Everything I made, I made for you. Take care not to ruin My world, because if you ruin it, there is no one to fix it after you.

So a broken toy is not thrown away at once. First we look at what broke, and try to fix it. If we cannot, we give the useful parts to someone who can use them. Only what is truly useless goes in the garbage. Guarding water, food and things is one way we show that the world belongs to Hashem.
--- words
trickles: flows slowly in small drops
gravel: small, loose stones
seeps: flows slowly through small openings
spring: a place where water flows out of the ground by itself
precious: very valuable and important
chemicals: materials, often strong, used to clean or make things
