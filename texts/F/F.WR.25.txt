At the end of the year, Rabbi Feld had a fair in the shul hall. Every boy brought something he made.

Naftali brought a clock he had built with Zeidy. It had a tiny gear that went round and round. When he wound it, it ticked gently.

Chaim brought a book of poems he had written. He read one out loud. He read it with the beat, and he took a tiny breath at every comma.

Avi brought a map of Shabbos Street. He had drawn every house, the corner store and the bridge by the river. He had to redraw the bridge twice. He knelt down to show everyone the river. He even added two puppies that hopped past the phone on the corner.
===
Dovid brought a plate of cookies. They were shaped like stars, and they smelled of cinnamon.

"Did you bake these by yourself?" asked Rabbi Feld.

"My mother helped," said Dovid. "But I did the shaping and the sprinkling. And I tasted only two."

Everybody laughed. Then Rabbi Feld stood up.

"This year, you learned to read hard words," he said. "You learned soft c, soft g and quiet letters. You learned endings and prefixes. You can read poems and plays. Now you can read almost anything."

The boys cheered. Then they shared Dovid's cookies, which was the best part of the fair.
--- words
fair: a big happy show where people share what they made
wound: turned to make it go
sprinkling: dropping tiny bits on top
shared: gave some to everyone
