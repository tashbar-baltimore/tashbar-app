By Mr. Levi Adler

Every fall, the trees on our street put on a show. Green leaves turn yellow, orange and bright red. Then they float down to the ground. Why does this happen?

A leaf is like a little kitchen. It makes food for the tree. To cook, the leaf needs sunlight, water and air. A green material inside the leaf catches the sunlight. This green material is called chlorophyll. Chlorophyll is what makes a leaf look green.
===
In the summer, the days are long and sunny. The leaf makes lots of chlorophyll, so the green covers up every other color.

In the fall, the days get shorter and the nights get cooler. The tree gets ready to rest for the winter. It stops making new chlorophyll. Little by little, the green fades away.

Now we can see colors that were hiding in the leaf all summer long. Yellow and orange were there the whole time! The green was just covering them up.
===
Some trees, like maples, also make a brand new color in the fall. Sugar gets trapped in their leaves, and the leaf uses it to make a new red color. That is why some maple trees turn bright red.

At last, a thin wall grows where the leaf meets the branch. The leaf cannot get water anymore. It dries up and falls to the ground.

The tree is not sick. It is resting. In the spring, new green leaves will grow, and the kitchen will start cooking again.
--- words
material: the stuff that something is made of
chlorophyll: the green material in a leaf that catches sunlight
fades: slowly gets lighter until it is gone
trapped: stuck inside, not able to get out
