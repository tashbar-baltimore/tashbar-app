The next morning, before Shacharis, Akiva drew a line down the middle of the whiteboard. On the left he wrote PROVED. On the right he wrote GUESSED.

"Rule of the newsroom," he said. "Nothing goes on the left side unless we saw it, or someone we trust saw it. Everything else goes on the right, and it stays there until we prove it."

Eli groaned. "You sound like my math teacher."

"Your math teacher is a wise man," said Akiva, and handed him the marker.

They began. On the PROVED side, Eli wrote: The 1951 to 1955 drawer is empty. Under it Gavriel added: A clean path in the dust, door to cabinet to chair. Yoni wrote: The door was locked when we came.

Then Eli started on the other side, and he wrote fast. Someone stole the files. Someone has a second key. Someone is hiding the yeshiva's history.

"Stole is a strong word," said Akiva. "Maybe someone borrowed them."

"Who borrows a whole drawer?"

"I don't know yet. That is why it is on the right side."
===
Gavriel had brought his camera. He scrolled through the pictures he had taken the day before, the clean path, the empty drawer, the chair by the window. He stopped on one and made it bigger.

"The dust on the path is thin," he said, "but it is not gone. There is a little dust on top of the footprints. If someone walked there years ago, the path would be gray again by now. If someone walked there this morning, there would be no dust at all."

"So?" said Eli.

"So someone has been walking there for a while. Weeks, maybe. Again and again."

Akiva wrote it on the GUESSED side, then stopped and looked at it. "Can we prove it?"

"Not yet," Gavriel admitted. "It is what the dust tells me. It is not a fact."

Benny sat at the end of the table. His hand was in his pocket, around the twist of clear paper. Twice he almost took it out. Twice somebody else spoke first, and the moment passed.
===
At lunch, Yoni went to Rabbi Rosen's office, because Yoni could ask anybody anything. He came back with his notebook open.

"Big news," he said. "There were always two keys to that room. Rabbi Brandt, the founder, kept one. The office kept the other. When Rabbi Brandt was niftar, in 1979, his key was never found. Everybody thought it was lost."

"So the second key is out there," said Eli. He picked up the marker again. "Then I know who it is. Mr. Dahan."

The room went quiet. Mr. Dahan was the custodian. He had worked at the yeshiva for two years, and he carried a ring of keys so big it jingled when he walked.

"He has keys to every door in the building," Eli went on. "He is in the basement every day. Who else could get in?"

Akiva took the marker out of Eli's hand, gently, and capped it. "Eli. Put a man's name next to the word stole, and that is not a guess anymore. That is a story about him. If we are wrong, it follows him around this building for years."

"I am not saying it is him. I am saying it could be."

"Then say it could be a lot of people," said Akiva. "And then we find out."
===
Eli folded his arms and stared at the board. He was not used to losing an argument, and he did not like it. But he did not write the name.

After school, Mr. Gross stopped by the closet. He had heard about the empty drawer, and he looked worried. "I keep the office key in my desk drawer," he said, "and my desk is locked at night. I checked it this morning. It is there, on its hook, where it always is."

"Has anyone else ever borrowed it?" asked Akiva.

"Not once in the eleven years I have been here." He paused at the door. "Boys, I want you to be careful about one more thing. Whoever took those folders knew exactly which drawer to open. Not the one from the sixties. Not the one from the seventies. The first one."

When he left, Yoni said what everyone was thinking. "So it is someone who cares about the beginning."

Benny stood up. His heart was beating faster than it should for something so small. He took the wrapper out of his pocket and laid it on the table.

"I found this," he said, "next to the chair by the window. It smells like peppermint." He took a breath. "Reb Feivel gave me a peppermint on the stairs, right before we went down. It is the same kind."

For a long moment, nobody said anything.

Then Akiva picked up the marker and walked to the board. He did not write a name. Under PROVED he wrote: A peppermint wrapper by the chair. And under GUESSED, in small letters, he wrote: Who eats peppermints?

"Good work, Benny," he said. "Next time, tell us the same day."
--- words

niftar: passed away
custodian: the man who takes care of a building
jingled: made a light ringing sound
capped: put the cap back on
