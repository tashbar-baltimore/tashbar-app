id: C68.MYS.01
title: The Missing Proofs
grade: 8
kind: play
topic: The Beacon newsroom
source: original
---
NARRATOR: It is Thursday evening in the Beacon office, a small room next to the yeshiva library. Tomorrow morning the printer picks up the final issue of the year. The five editors have stayed late to check the last pages.

AKIVA: (firmly) All right, everyone. One last look at the page proofs, and then we go home. Gavriel, where are they?

GAVRIEL: (puzzled) I left them right here on the long table, in the blue folder. Twelve pages, every one with my corrections in red.

YONI: (looking around) The table is empty. There is a coffee ring, two pencils and a stapler. No blue folder.

ELI: (suspicious) Then someone took it. And I can think of one person who was in this room alone this afternoon.

BENNY: (surprised) Me? I came in to return the stapler. I was here for two minutes.

ELI: (sharply) Two minutes is enough to pick up a folder.
===
NARRATOR: The audience should know something that the boys do not. At five o'clock, Mr. Gross, the faculty adviser, walked in, saw the blue folder and smiled. "I will read these on the bus," he said to the empty room, and he slipped the folder into his briefcase. Then he went home.

AKIVA: (calmly) Eli, slow down. A newspaper does not print a story without proof, and we do not blame a person without proof either.

ELI: (insisting) Then explain this. Benny said last week that he wished his story were on the front page instead of mine. Now the front page is gone.

BENNY: (hurt) I said I wished it. I did not say I would steal it.

GAVRIEL: (quietly) There is something else. The folder was on the table at four thirty. I am sure of it, because I wrote the time on the last page.

YONI: (thinking) And Benny left at four.

ELI: (less sure) Are you certain?

YONI: (nodding) I walked out with him. We said Minchah together at four fifteen.
===
NARRATOR: The boys search the shelves, the trash can and the drawer under the window. Eli searches the hardest of all.

AKIVA: (thinking aloud) Who else has a key to this room?

GAVRIEL: (slowly) Only the five of us. And Mr. Gross.

ELI: (relieved, then embarrassed) Mr. Gross! He always takes our pages home to read on the bus. Benny, I owe you an apology. I accused you because I was angry, not because I had a reason.

BENNY: (warmly) Accepted. You were worried about the paper. So was I.

AKIVA: (smiling) Let us call him before we jump to the next conclusion.

NARRATOR: Akiva dials. The phone rings once.

MR. GROSS: (cheerfully, on the phone) Good evening, editors! I have your proofs right here. Page seven has a spelling mistake in the second headline, and page nine is the best page you have ever made.

ALL: (laughing with relief) Thank you, Mr. Gross!

ELI: (to Benny) Next year, I am putting your story on the front page myself.
--- words
proofs: the first printed copies of pages, checked for mistakes before the real printing
suspicious: thinking someone did something wrong
accused: said that someone did something wrong
conclusion: what you decide after thinking about the facts
adviser: a grown-up who guides a group and helps it make good choices
