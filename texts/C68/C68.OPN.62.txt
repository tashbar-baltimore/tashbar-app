To our reporter,

You proved it. You can find the words that back up an answer, tell what is said from what is hinted, and explain every step of your thinking. Now we go one level deeper.

Every good story is about two things at once. On top, it is about what happens: a locked room, a missing drawer, an old man with a key. Underneath, it is about something true about life. That second thing is called the theme, and the author almost never says it out loud. He shows it through what the characters want, what stands in their way, and how they change.

In this unit you will learn five small skills, one at a time:
how character, conflict and theme fit together;
how to tell a theme from a topic or a moral;
how a plot unfolds, one episode at a time;
how a character responds to trouble and changes;
and how to sum up a story fairly, without your own opinion in it.

You will use them on the second half of The Beacon and the Locked Archive, where the real secret of the archive comes out, and on a story about a boy, a promise and a Gemara.

Here is my one request. When you finish a story, do not only ask, "What happened?" Ask, "What is this story trying to teach me?" Then prove your answer, the way you learned in Unit 6.1.

Your editor,
Akiva Feldman
