An editorial by Akiva Feldman, editor.

This year, a question has divided our newsroom. Should The Beacon stop printing on paper and appear only online? Eli Mizrahi, our sports editor, says yes. I say no. Before I explain why, I want to give Eli's side the fair hearing it deserves, because it is a strong side.

Eli's first reason is money. Printing costs the yeshiva about eight hundred dollars a year. That money could buy seforim for the library or a new table for the lunchroom. His second reason is speed. Online, a story about Sunday's game could appear on Sunday night, not two weeks later. His third reason is waste. Every issue uses three hundred sheets of paper, and some copies end up on the floor of the bus.

I agree with Eli on one point completely: we waste too many copies. We should print fewer, perhaps two hundred instead of three hundred, and stop leaving piles on the bus. That change alone would save about a third of the paper and a good part of the cost.
===
But I do not agree that the answer is to give up paper altogether. Here is why.

First, a paper copy reaches people that a website does not. Many of our grandparents read The Beacon at the Shabbos table. Rabbi Rosen, our menahel, told me that his father keeps every issue in a drawer. A website cannot be read on Shabbos, and a link is not something a Zaidy keeps in a drawer.

Second, Eli says online is faster. That is true, but faster is not always better for a newspaper like ours. We are not a news service racing to be first. Our job is to be careful. Every story in The Beacon is checked twice, by the writer and by an editor, before it is printed. When a story can be posted in five minutes, the checking is the first thing to go.

Third, consider the money. Eight hundred dollars is real money, and I do not want to wave it away. But a website is not free either. Someone must build it, keep it running and fix it when it breaks. Mr. Gross, our adviser, asked a company for a price. The answer was about six hundred dollars a year. If we print fewer copies, the paper Beacon will cost only a little more than a website would.

Some readers will say that I love paper because I am old-fashioned. Maybe I am. But this is not a question of what I love. It is a question of who we write for. We write for the whole yeshiva family: the boys, their parents and their grandparents.
===
So here is my proposal. We print fewer copies, so the waste stops. We post a short online page each week with scores and dates, so Eli's speed problem is solved. And we keep the full Beacon on paper, checked twice, ready for the Shabbos table.

Eli and I will keep arguing about this, as good editors should. But on one thing we agree: a newspaper that listens to the other side is a newspaper worth reading.
===
Eli answers: a reply by Eli Mizrahi, sports editor.

Akiva has written a fair editorial, and I want to thank him for it. He stated my reasons better than I did. Now I want to answer three of his points.

He says a paper copy reaches our grandparents. That is true, and I had not thought of it. My own Zaidy reads every issue at the Shabbos table, and I would not want to take that away from him. So I accept this point. Any plan we make must keep something on paper for the Shabbos table.

He says faster is not better, because quick posting means careless checking. Here I disagree. Speed and care are not enemies. A score is a score. The game ended, the numbers are known, and there is nothing to check twice. I agree that a long story needs two careful readers. A box of scores does not.

He says a website costs about six hundred dollars a year. That number surprised me, so I asked my cousin, who builds websites. He said a simple page like ours could cost much less, perhaps one hundred dollars a year, if a student keeps it up to date. I have asked Mr. Gross to get a second price before we decide.

In the end, Akiva and I are closer than our headlines make it look. We both want fewer wasted copies. We both want the Shabbos table to keep its Beacon. The only real question left is how much to spend, and that question will be answered by facts, not by who argues louder.

Until then, I will keep writing for the paper Beacon, and he will keep reading my scores online.
