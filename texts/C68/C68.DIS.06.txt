title: After the Water
grade: 8
kind: poem
topic: Disasters and how people rebuild
source: original
---
The river took the bridge at night,
the bakery, the school,
the bench where Zaidy sat each day
beside the shady pool.

It took the photographs, the chairs,
the seforim on the shelf.
It left the mud, and left the town
to find its way itself.

At dawn the neighbors came with brooms,
with buckets, bread and tea.
A boy I'd never spoken to
began to work with me.

We scraped the floors. We hauled the wood.
We sorted what was found.
We sang the way my father sings
when hope is hard to sound.
===
They say the water rose ten feet.
They say it rose in an hour.
They say a town is only stone,
and stone gives way to power.

But I have seen what rivers miss,
what flood can never take:
the hands that reach, the hands that hold,
the hands that help remake.

So when they build the bridge again,
and paint the school bright blue,
I'll know that what was strongest here
was never stone. It's you.
--- words
seforim: holy books
hauled: pulled or carried something heavy
remake: to make something again
