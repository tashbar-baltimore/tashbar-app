Good morning, boys. My name is Dr. Avraham Sasson, and for thirty years my job has been to listen to the earth.

When people hear that I study earthquakes, they always ask the same question: can you tell us when the next big one will come? And every time, I have to give the same honest answer, which is that no one, not me and not any scientist in the world, can tell you the day or the hour that the ground will move.

But here is what we can do, and it matters more than you might think.

We can tell you where the ground is most likely to shake, because the plates that grind against each other have left their marks on the land for thousands of years. We can tell you how strong the shaking might be, because the rocks remember the earthquakes of the past. And we can tell a city, years before anything happens, which buildings will stand and which ones need help.

When a large earthquake does strike under the ocean, our network of seismographs can find it within a few minutes, and in those minutes, a warning can travel faster than the wave itself, so that a family on the shore has time to climb to higher ground.

So when you read about an earthquake, and you see the pictures of the broken roads and the fallen walls, remember that somewhere, quietly, a needle was already drawing the shape of it, and someone was already learning from it, so that next time, more people would be safe.

That, boys, is what the needle knows. Thank you.
