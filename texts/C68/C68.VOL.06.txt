id: C68.VOL.06
title: The Quiet Ground
grade: 6
kind: poem
topic: Volcanoes and earthquakes
source: original
---
We call it solid, the ground beneath,
we call it still, we call it sure;
we build our houses on its back
and think that it will just endure.

But deep below the cellar floor,
below the roots, below the stone,
the mantle turns in giant rings
as slow as moss, and not alone.

The plates drift on, an inch a year,
as quiet as a growing nail;
they press and lock and hold their breath
and store their strength in every scale.

And then one morning, with no knock,
the held breath breaks, the long rock slips;
the chimneys fall, the windows sing,
the bay runs backward from the ships.

So engineers with careful hands
build walls that bend and frames that sway,
and someone in a quiet room
reads lines a needle drew today.

We cannot stop the turning earth,
nor tell the morning it will start;
but we can learn, and build, and watch,
and keep a listening, patient heart.
