title: The Friend We Never Knew (Rabbi Rosen's words)

Rabbotai, boys, dear families.

Seventy-five years ago, this yeshiva was two rented rooms over a bakery. Sixty boys. One rebbe who was also the principal, the bookkeeper and, I am told, the man who swept the floor.

In the spring of 1953, those two rooms almost closed. The rent was not paid. The teachers were not paid. The landlord wrote a letter, and the letter said: by the end of May.

And then, one Thursday night, an envelope came under the door. Inside it, eighteen dollars. No name. No note. Only the number, in pencil.

The next Thursday, another. And the next. For two years.

This month, five boys in a supply closet found out who sent them. His name was Yisroel Mandel. He was a tailor on Lombard Street. He had lost everyone he loved, and he lived on bread and tea, and every week he gave eighteen dollars to a school for other people's children.

He never told anyone. He never wanted thanks.

So tonight, we will not thank him with speeches. We will thank him the way he thanked the world. Quietly. Every week. Without our names.

Starting this Thursday, there will be a box in the front hall. The Mandel Fund. Eighteen dollars a week, from all of us together, for a family in our city who needs it.

Seventy-five years ago, a friend we never knew kept our doors open.

Let us keep his door open now.
