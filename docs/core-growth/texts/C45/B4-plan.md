# Book B4 · *The Map in Zeidy's Attic* (Grade 5, 12 chapters) · plan

Job C45. Units 5.1–5.3. Cast: the Glen Avenue Crew (`docs/core-growth/C45-map.md` §5), now in Grade 5, age 11. Draft texts; they move to job T's content store when it exists. Chapters 1–6 are written (`texts/C45/C45.B4.01–06.txt`); chapters 7–12 are planned here.

**The story in one line:** On Chanukah, Yehuda finds his great-great-grandfather's hand-drawn map of the 1904 Baltimore Fire in Zeidy's attic, and the Glen Avenue Crew follows its star to find out what Mottel Rosen carried out of the fire, and where it is now, without hurting the Zeidy who has carried a sad secret for sixty years.

**Matches the opening passage** (`src/CoreDataC452.html`, `c45_attic`): one box a year at Chanukah; a flat wooden box, rusty lock, key taped underneath; a faded tallis bag; a map of old Baltimore in brown ink with a tiny star near the harbor and three Hebrew letters; Zeidy's singing stops; the photo of a young man in a cap in front of a burned building, dated February 1904 in the same brown ink.

**Theme (Unit 5.2):** How a person meets a challenge shows who he is. Mottel ran toward the fire; Yehuda must meet a quieter challenge: to honor his Zeidy and stop when asked.
**Characters who change each other (Unit 5.3):** Yehuda and Zeidy. Yehuda learns patience ("a question is like hot soup"); Zeidy, through Yehuda, stops saying "next week". Also Tzvi Hirsch learns from Nosson and Binyamin that courage can mean waiting.
**Turning point (Unit 5.3):** ch. 8. Zeidy reads the words under the tape, learns his Zeidy left them for him, and decides: "Not next week. Today." The same chapter turns the mystery: ספר means *sapar*, barber.

**The family:** Zeidy Benaim's father came from Morocco; his mother was born in Baltimore to a Lithuanian family (so "Zeidy" and "Savta"). Her father was **Mottel (Mordechai) Rosen**, born about 1886, a cap-maker, who died when Zeidy was ten (about 1962).

**What really happened (the answer):** Sunday, February 7, 1904. Mottel, 18, ran downtown to the Hollander & Sons cap building on German Street, where the Cap-Makers' Minyan davened in a top-floor room. He carried out its small sefer Torah wrapped in his tallis (sparks scorched the bottom ends of the wooden handles, and the corner of his tallis bag, but "not one letter touched the fire"). Streets west were burning, so he went east, refused a wagon, crossed a bridge over the Jones Falls, and left it overnight in the back room of **Reb Zalman Fine, a barber (*sapar*) on Thames Street** near the harbor, on the side that did not burn. In March 1904 the shul Ohr Chodosh in East Baltimore took in the minyan and its sefer Torah. Decades later, when Ohr Chodosh closed (1962), Mottel arranged for the sefer Torah to go to the boys' own yeshiva. He wanted to show ten-year-old Zeidy that Sunday; Zeidy said "next week"; Mottel passed away that Thursday. Mottel had written the answer in Yiddish under the key tape: *"It slept at the sapar's, Zalman Fine, Thames Street. Where it is read now, ask his son. For my grandson, if I cannot show you myself."* It is the yeshiva's oldest sefer Torah, the one Avrumi's Abba is repairing, with handles "black at the bottom, like burnt toast."

All names of people, shuls and businesses in the family story (Mottel Rosen, Hollander & Sons, the Cap-Makers' Minyan, Ohr Chodosh, Reb Zalman Fine, Mr. Pollack, *The Messenger*) are invented. Only the fire's facts are real.

4. The Water Stain: the museum archivist and the Ohr Chodosh minute book; "carried out of the fire with his own hands"; which source is stronger and why; "the shop of Reb Z..." under a water stain. *Hook: the forgotten duck-head umbrella: "That's my Zeidy's."*
5. Next Week: Shabbos at Zeidy and Savta's (spicy fish, Kiddush, zemiros); Yehuda throws his questions; Zeidy leaves the table; Savta's story of "next week"; "a question is like hot soup"; Yehuda apologizes; Zeidy does not sing. *Hook: after Havdalah: "Put it back. Lock it. Some things should stay lost."*
6. Under the Tape: the crew's temptation (photograph it and search in secret); Nosson: "a present wrapped in a secret"; Rabbi Feldman asks Rav Mizrachi; the Rav's answer; Tzvi Hirsch: "Maybe sometimes brave means not doing something." In the attic, Nosson's sideways light shows Yiddish words under the old tape, with ס פ ר and a zayin. *Hook: Yehuda's hand moves toward his pencil, then stops.*
7. The Promise (planned): Yehuda does not copy the words. He tapes the key, locks the box, and tells the crew the hard truth; Tzvi Hirsch is angry, then stands by him. Avrumi's Abba lets the class see the sefer Torah repair (the burnt handles, close up; nobody connects it yet). At a calm moment, Yehuda tells Zeidy with respect what is in his heart, and that there is writing under the tape, and then he listens. *Hook: Zeidy, at night, on the phone: "Bring the key. And bring your friends."*
8. Not Next Week (TURNING POINT): Zeidy reads the Yiddish aloud and learns the words were left for him; he cries and then laughs. He explains ספר as *sapar* from the Mishnah his Zeidy taught him ("a person should not sit before the barber close to Mincha"); Tzvi Hirsch's joke was right. Zeidy: "I said next week once. Not again. Today." *Hook: Avrumi lays his copy over a modern map: the star sits on a building on Thames Street that is still standing.*
9. Thames Street: Zeidy and the crew in Fells Point; the old building (now a shop) with an old iron bracket where a barber pole once hung; a neighbor sends them to the barber's grandson. Unit 5.3: how the setting (water, the safe side, an old street) shapes what happened. *Hook: an old man opens the door: "Rosen? My father said a Rosen would come one day."*
10. The Barber's Grandson: Mr. Leibel Fine, 93, has a 1962 letter from Mottel to his father: the sefer Torah of Ohr Chodosh is going "to the yeshiva, where boys will read from it." Avrumi goes pale: the burnt handles. *Hook: Avrumi: "I know where it is. I see it every day."*
11. Not One Letter: in the beis medrash, Avrumi's Abba shows the scorched handles; under the old mantle's lining is a tiny gold-stitched cap, like the cap on the map. The sofer reports the parchment has no fire damage; whether it is kosher for reading is checked by the sofer and decided by the Rav. Zeidy holds the handles in his hands. *Hook: the Rav says the repair will be finished for this Shabbos, and the gabbai asks Zeidy to come.*
12. Zeidy Sings: Shabbos in the yeshiva's shul; the sefer Torah is read; the gabbai calls Zeidy for an aliyah; Mr. Fine comes too; a kiddush with cake from Tzvi Hirsch's family's bakery. Walking home, Zeidy sings again. Yehuda writes in his notebook: *How a person meets a challenge shows who he is.* THE END.

## Review notes for chapters 7–12 (independent review, 2026-11-24)
- Ch. 7–12 must answer: why the box was in Zeidy's own attic without his knowing (it came with his mother's things when she moved in, unopened); why Zeidy's mother knew nothing (Mottel "never liked to be praised"); why Zeidy never saw the sefer Torah at the yeshiva after 1962 (it was kept in the small beis medrash upstairs and read only on Mondays and Thursdays).
- Use the third reading of ספר (*sofer*, Avrumi's Abba is one) as a second red herring.
- Ch. 8 must lift all blame: a ten-year-old did nothing wrong, and Mottel's note "if I cannot show you myself" shows he planned for it.
- Zeidy explains that Mottel used the Mishnah's word *sapar*, not the Yiddish *sherer*.
- A grown-up says plainly, once more: in real life you never go into a fire.
- The last page of the book says: "The 1904 fire is real. The people, shops, shuls, newspaper and history society in this story are made up."
- Pictures: no Savta, Ima, sisters or librarian (words only).
