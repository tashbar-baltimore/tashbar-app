# The Missing Esrog · Chapter 3: Leaves on the Stairs

For a second, nobody breathed.

Nosson stood next to their desks. His voice was quiet, but his hands were shaking a little.

"I did not take the esrog," he said. "I would never take something from a rebbe."

Tzvi Hirsch opened his mouth. Binyamin kicked his chair, gently, under the desk. Tzvi Hirsch closed his mouth.

"Then where did the flax come from?" Yehuda asked. He tried to say it kindly, the way his Abba asked questions, not the way people on the news asked questions.

Nosson looked down at his sleeve as if he were seeing the fluff for the first time. "This? From this morning. Before davening, the rebbe was unpacking the esrog in the hall. It came in a box full of this stuff. He asked me to carry the box to the garbage." He brushed at his cuff. "It sticks to everything."

Yehuda wrote that down. Then he looked up. "Why were your hands behind your back when you came in?"

Nosson's ears turned red. "Because I was holding the rebbe's magnifying glass, and everybody was staring at me, and I didn't want anyone to think I took *that*." He sighed. "I guess it didn't work. The office was locked, so I waited on the bench outside it for Mrs. Rivkin until the bell rang."

Avrumi laughed, a short surprised laugh, and then Binyamin smiled, and even Nosson smiled a little. Tzvi Hirsch did not smile. But he stopped frowning, which for Tzvi Hirsch was almost the same thing.

"Mrs. Rivkin said to read," said Binyamin. "We have nine minutes before she comes back. Who wants to look for clues?"

Five hands went up. Then six. Nosson had raised his too.

They worked fast and quietly. Avrumi held his map and marked each place they checked. Dovid crawled under the rebbe's desk with the magnifying glass. Binyamin checked the hooks. Yehuda stood in the middle of the room and turned slowly in a circle, looking at everything, the way Zeidy Benaim said a person should look at a new city.

"The radiator," said Dovid from the floor. "The papers that blew off are stuck behind it. I can see the edges."

"Can you reach them?"

Dovid stretched his arm. "No. It's too hot. Mr. Gomez just fixed it." He blew on his fingers.

Yehuda wrote *papers behind radiator, too hot* and drew another star.

Then Nosson, who was standing by the door, said, "Come look at this."

The classroom was on the second floor, near the end of the hall. Right outside the door were the stairs. One set went down, to the yard and the front door. The other went up, to the third floor, where the Rav of the school, Rav Mizrachi, had his office and his library of tall old seforim. Boys were not allowed upstairs without a reason.

On the stairs going up, there were leaves.

Not many. A red one on the first step. A brown, crumpled one on the third. A wet orange one, pressed flat, on the fifth, as if a shoe had stepped on it and carried it there.

"From the yard," said Avrumi, kneeling. "Leaves come in on everyone's shoes."

"But all of us came up from the yard and stopped here," said Yehuda. "Nobody from our class went higher." He pointed back through the door at the wet orange leaves under the open window, by the rebbe's desk. "Same kind. Somebody walked from our room and went **up**."

Avrumi drew a little arrow on his map, pointing up the stairs. Under it he wrote: *Who?*

Yehuda's mind was racing. He wrote his evidence very fast:

*Hat gone, coat still here = he went somewhere close. Not home.*
*Cloth folded, smells fresh = the esrog was taken out carefully.*
*Mr. Gomez talked to the rebbe = the rebbe is still in the building.*
*Leaves go UP.*

He looked at the four lines. Then he looked at the stairs.

"I think," he said slowly, "the rebbe went upstairs. And I think he took the esrog with him."

"Why would he do that?" asked Tzvi Hirsch. "And why wouldn't he tell anyone?"

"I don't know yet," Yehuda admitted. "But all the clues point the same way."

Binyamin nodded. "In chess, when all your pieces point at one square, that is where the game is."

"We're not allowed upstairs," said Dovid.

"We're allowed to ask a question," said Nosson. "That's a reason."

They looked at one another. Then, before anyone could change his mind, the six boys climbed the stairs, stepping around the leaves so they would not spoil the clues.

The third-floor hall was quiet and smelled of old books. At the end of it was a dark wooden door with a brass sign: *Rav Mizrachi*. The door was closed.

Yehuda lifted his hand to knock.

Then he froze.

From behind the door came two voices. One was deep and slow, and Yehuda did not know it. The other was Rabbi Feldman's.

"I saw it right away," Rabbi Feldman was saying, and he sounded worried. "A spot. A tiny dark spot. If it is what I think it is, then—"

The deep voice answered something the boys could not hear.

Then Rabbi Feldman said, very clearly, "Then the esrog is ruined, and the boys must never find out what I did."

Six boys stared at one another in the silent hall.

Behind them, on the stairs, someone was coming up.
