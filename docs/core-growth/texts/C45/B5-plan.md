# Book B5 · *Shabbos at the Lighthouse* (Grade 5, 12 chapters) · plan

C45 helper (claim #445). Units 5.4–5.6 (`docs/core-growth/C45-map.md`). Cast: the Glen Avenue Crew (`C45-map.md` §5), Grade 5, age 11. Texts are in `texts/C45/C45.B5.01–12.txt`, and a boy reads them through the Book Club lessons in `src/CoreDataC45H3.html` (one lesson per chapter).

**The story in one line:** On a Friday off from school, Tzvi Hirsch Gold and three friends go out with his great-uncle, Reb Shimon, to help fix up an old lighthouse in the Chesapeake Bay. A storm comes early, and they must make Shabbos on a little house on iron legs in the middle of the water. Then, in the night, the light goes dark, and a fishing boat is out there.

**Theme:** Gratitude keeps a light burning. Reb Shimon cares for the light because it once saved him, and the boys learn that the people who help us deserve to be remembered and thanked (*hakaras hatov*).

**Who is who:**
- **Tzvi Hirsch Gold** (lead): funny, brave, sometimes too fast; his family runs the kosher bakery. **Nosson Abergel** notices more than he says. **Dovid Lerner** fixes things. **Binyamin Yusupov** is calm, and thinks like a chess player. Yehuda and Avrumi are at home and come in at ch. 10–11 (they know the 1904 fire from Book B4).
- **Reb Shimon Gold**: Tzvi Hirsch's great-uncle, about 75, a retired ship's engineer with a white beard and a soft voice. He is the volunteer keeper of the lighthouse museum. He says, "The bay keeps its own schedule."
- **Captain Ray Turner and his son Cody (13)**: the crew of the fishing boat *Miss Ellie*.
- **The Coast Guard**: voices on the radio, and a rescue boat.

**The lighthouse:** Kettle Shoal Light, a white six-sided wooden cottage with a red roof and a lantern room on top. It stands on iron screwpile legs, five miles out in the bay, south of Baltimore. Built in 1883, it is now automatic: a Coast Guard beacon flashes white every 6 seconds, and the cottage is a museum. Kettle Shoal Light is made up. Screwpile lighthouses are real (Thomas Point Shoal Light is one).

**How the chapters are told:**
- Third person, following Tzvi Hirsch, except where noted.
- Ch. 7 is built differently: Reb Shimon's log entries, with times, carry the night.
- Ch. 8 is Reb Shimon's story, in his own words (a first-person story inside the chapter).
- Ch. 9 is the same night, told by Captain Turner.
- Ch. 10 sets four accounts side by side (the newspaper, the Coast Guard, the log, the captain).

**Chapters (≈ 1,150–1,350 words each; each ends on a hook):**
1. **The Light on Iron Legs.** A Friday off from school, the week after Purim. The workboat *Shoshana* leaves the harbor at 7:30. Ima's "just in case" box, which Tzvi Hirsch thinks is silly. The lighthouse comes into view: what a screwpile lighthouse is and why it stands on legs. The forecast says the storm comes Saturday night. *Hook: at 11:00 Reb Shimon taps the barometer. The needle is falling fast. "That storm," he says, "is not waiting for Motzaei Shabbos."*
2. **The Bay Keeps Its Own Schedule.** The work: scraping and painting, and Dovid's fix of the old fog bell. Whitecaps. At noon they try to leave; the waves are too steep for a small boat. The Coast Guard, on the radio: stay put; the lighthouse is the safest place. Phone calls home before Shabbos. *Hook: Reb Shimon looks at the box. "Boys, it seems we are making Shabbos here."*
3. **Erev Shabbos at Sea.** Getting ready with what they have. Tides and storm surge, explained by Reb Shimon at the window: the water climbs the iron legs. The keepers who lived here with their families long ago. Reb Shimon lights the Shabbos candles. *Hook: as the match goes out, a wave booms under the floor, higher than any wave all day.*
4. **A Lighthouse Shabbos.** Kabbalas Shabbos with the wind; Kiddush on grape juice; Ima's chicken and the bakery's challah. Zemiros louder than the storm, verse by verse. Reb Shimon begins to tell why he loves this light, then stops: "Another time." *Hook: the white beam that has swept past the window every 6 seconds does not come back.*
5. **The Dark Light.** The automatic beacon has failed. The radio: the fishing boat *Miss Ellie*, with engine trouble, is somewhere south. Reb Shimon explains *pikuach nefesh*: when a life may be in danger, we do what is needed, even on Shabbos, and his Rav taught him long ago what to do in exactly this case. He climbs to the lantern room and turns on the backup lamp. The boys watch, count the flashes and keep a lookout. *Hook: through the rain, a tiny green light, low on the water, moving toward the shoal.*
6. **Green Light on the Water.** Green means the boat's right side. Binyamin works out that it is drifting toward the shallow shoal. Reb Shimon on the radio with the Coast Guard; the boys at the window with binoculars, calling out what they see. The Coast Guard boat is coming. *Hook: the green light is gone.*
7. **The Keeper's Log** (built from Reb Shimon's log entries, with times). The long night, hour by hour: the rescue boat's searchlight, *Miss Ellie* found stuck on the edge of the shoal, the captain and his son safe; the boys asleep on the floor; dawn. Shacharis at the window; a calm gray bay full of floating logs. Shabbos morning, Nosson reads the old keeper's logbook on the shelf. *Hook: the entry for February 7, 1904, says "A great red glow to the northwest all night." In the margin, in newer pencil: "S.G. 1971: this light saved my life."*
8. **S.G.** Shabbos afternoon. Reb Shimon tells his story in his own words: 1971, a young engine-room hand on a cargo ship in fog, and the light and bell of Kettle Shoal that warned them off the shoal. He calls the light "an old friend". *Hook: at seudah shlishis, a boat horn: the Coast Guard boat pulls up, and someone on board wants to meet the boys.*
9. **The Captain's Story** (the same night, told by Captain Turner after Havdalah). His fear, his son, the dead engine, the dark where the light should have been, and the white flash every 6 seconds that told him exactly where he was. *Hook: "If your light hadn't come back on at 9:30, we would have hit those iron legs." Tzvi Hirsch frowns: the log says 9:15.*
10. **Four Accounts.** Sunday in Baltimore. The crew lays out four accounts of one night:
    - the *Bay Courier* newspaper story ("a volunteer and four boys kept the light burning");
    - the Coast Guard's short report;
    - Reb Shimon's log;
    - Captain Turner's words.

    They agree on some things and differ on others. Yehuda asks "but how do we know?" and Avrumi makes a chart. *Hook: Reb Shimon calls. The museum wants one account for its wall. Which one?*
11. **The 1904 Glow.** Avrumi and Yehuda recognize the date from Book B4. The 1904 keeper saw only a glow, from five miles away, and wrote only what he saw; the newspapers wrote what others told them. Why accounts differ: who wrote it, where they stood, what they saw, and why they wrote it. The crew decides that the wall should show several accounts side by side. *Hook: Captain Turner calls. He is coming to the opening, and he is bringing something that belongs to the lighthouse.*
12. **The Wall of Many Eyes.** The museum night (a weekday evening). Captain Turner and Cody bring the lighthouse's old brass fog bell clapper, which they found tangled in *Miss Ellie*'s net that morning. The wall shows each account, and Tzvi Hirsch's own careful one. Reb Shimon adds a new line in the margin of the old log: "This light, and four boys, helped two more get home." THE END. The last page notes that Kettle Shoal Light, its people and the boat are made up; screwpile lighthouses and the 1904 fire are real.

**Unit fit:**
- **Unit 5.4, big ideas in articles** (ch. 1–3): each chapter carries real ocean knowledge (screwpile lighthouses, waves and whitecaps, tides and storm surge). The lessons use summarize with big ideas and key details, link two ideas (a cause and its effect), and explain with the text's own details.
- **Unit 5.5, how texts are built** (ch. 4–7): how a chapter fits the whole book (ch. 4, 5); stanzas and scenes (the zemiros verse by verse, and ch. 6's scenes at the window); compare how two parts are built (ch. 7's log against the chapters before it).
- **Unit 5.6, whose story is it?** (ch. 8–12): the narrator's view colors the story (ch. 8: "an old friend"); same scene, another narrator (ch. 9 against ch. 5–6); one event, many accounts (ch. 10); why accounts differ (ch. 11, with the 1904 log).
