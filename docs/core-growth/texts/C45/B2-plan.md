# Book B2 · *Lost on the Ridge Trail* (Grade 4, 10 chapters) · plan

C45 helper (claim #445). Units 4.4–4.6 (`docs/core-growth/C45-map.md`). Cast: the Glen Avenue Crew (`C45-map.md` §5), Grade 4, age 10. Texts are in `texts/C45/C45.B2.01–10.txt`, and a boy reads them through the Book Club lessons in `src/CoreDataC45H3.html` (one lesson per chapter, below).

**The story in one line:** On a bunk hike at Camp Pine Lake, a sudden thunderstorm splits Tzvi Hirsch Gold and Avrumi Katz from their group. The fast boy and the careful boy must spend a night on the mountain, and they get found because they *stop running and start thinking*.

**The same world as the C45 passages** (`src/CoreDataC459.html`): Camp Pine Lake, Reb Akiva the counselor, Ridge Trail with its blue marks, cairns on the way to the lookout, Avrumi's map and compass, Tzvi Hirsch who runs ahead. Avrumi hiked Ridge Trail once before with his Abba (passage `c45_rcmarks`), so he knows what the blue marks mean.

**Theme (Unit 4.3 skills used again; Unit 4.5 summaries end with it):** Stop and think before you run. When you are lost, the bravest thing is to stay put, stay together and help people find you.

**Real-life safety (said plainly in the book, ch. 3, 4 and 10):** If you are lost, stay where you are, keep together, blow your whistle three times, and make yourself easy to see. Never climb toward lightning, never shelter under the one tall tree, and never walk at night. The grown-ups and the rangers do the searching. The boys are found because they follow these rules.

**Who is who (new in B2):**
- **Reb Akiva Brenner**: the bunk counselor, 19, calm, a whistle around his neck. (Already in the C45 passages.)
- **Rabbi Weiss**: the camp director.
- **Ranger Dan Morales and his search dog, Scout**: the park ranger who leads the search.
- **Nosson Abergel** and **Dovid Lerner** are in the same bunk. Nosson saw which way the cap blew (the clue that sends the search west). Dovid has the camp drone club. Yehuda and Binyamin are at camp too, in another bunk; B2 is Tzvi Hirsch and Avrumi's book.

**How the chapters are told (point of view matters for Unit 4.6):**
- Ch. 1–7 and 10: third person, following Tzvi Hirsch and Avrumi.
- Ch. 8: **first person**, Tzvi Hirsch's own account, written in his camp notebook the night after ("My side of the story"). He tells what he felt and thought, and he does not know what happened at camp.
- Ch. 9: third person, following **Reb Akiva at camp** during the same night. It includes a short article from the camp newspaper, *The Pine Lake Ripple*, written by a boy who was not there (a **secondhand** account). The article gets one big thing wrong: it says the boys "wandered for miles", but they stayed put.
- Ch. 10: the two boys compare the accounts at the campfire and set the record straight.

**Chapters (≈ 900–1,100 words each; each ends on a hook):**
1. **Twenty Blue Marks.** Monday at Camp Pine Lake. Bunk Daled's hike to Eagle Rock, the lookout on Ridge Trail. Reb Akiva's three rules: stay in sight of the boy in front, follow the blue marks, three whistle blasts mean "help". Tzvi Hirsch counts blue marks and runs ahead; Avrumi draws the trail. At breakfast the sky had small puffy clouds; by lunch at the creek they are taller than the mountain. *Hook: Avrumi points west at a cloud "flat on top, like an anvil." Reb Akiva's face changes. "We turn back. Now."*
2. **The Anvil Cloud.** The wind turns cold; the first rumble; Reb Akiva leads the bracha on thunder and hurries them down. A gust takes Tzvi Hirsch's red cap off the trail and down a slope; he runs after it without a word. Avrumi follows to bring him back. Rain like a wall; the map tears. When they climb back up, the trail is not where they left it. *Hook: they turn in a full circle. There is not one blue mark on any tree.*
3. **Count the Seconds.** Lightning. Avrumi remembers what his Abba taught him: get off the high ground, away from the tallest tree, crouch low on your pack. They count flash to bang: 3 seconds, then 6, then 12 (the storm is moving away). The bracha on lightning. Three whistle blasts, again and again; only thunder answers. Tzvi Hirsch wants to run downhill to find camp. Avrumi says the hardest word: "Stop." *Hook: something big is crashing through the bushes, straight toward them.*
4. **What We Have.** It is a deer, as scared as they are. The boys laugh, then get serious: STOP (Stop, Think, Observe, Plan). They decide to stay where they are. Inventory: half a water bottle, trail mix, two squashed rugelach from the Gold bakery, a flashlight, a compass, a whistle each, the orange poncho. They hang the poncho high on a branch. Avrumi draws a new map from memory. They daven Mincha, using the compass to face east. *Hook: the sun drops behind the ridge, and the flashlight flickers and goes dark.*
5. **The Longest Night.** A dry spot under a rock ledge. Cold, wet socks, strange sounds. Tzvi Hirsch admits he is scared, and that it is his fault. Avrumi tells how his Abba the sofer fixes a mistake: one careful letter at a time. They say Shema together. The clouds clear; Avrumi finds the Big Dipper and the North Star, so they know which way is north. *Hook: far below, through the trees, a light blinks three times. Then three more.*
6. **Signals.** Is it someone looking for them? They answer with three blasts. The light blinks again, then is gone. Tzvi Hirsch starts toward it in the dark; this time he stops himself. They take turns sleeping. At dawn there is fog. Next to the ledge is a bare rock as big as a classroom floor, and they spend an hour building a giant arrow of white stones pointing to their ledge. *Hook: a high buzzing sound grows louder above the fog.*
7. **The Hum in the Sky.** A search drone with a blinking red light circles over the stone arrow and the orange poncho, hovers, dips once, and flies away. Did it see them? Tzvi Hirsch wants to follow it. He looks at Avrumi, and stays. They wait, whistle and wait. *Hook: an orange-vested dog bursts out of the bushes, barking, and a man's voice calls, "Tzvi Hirsch Gold! Avrumi Katz!"*
8. **My Side of the Story** (first person: Tzvi Hirsch's notebook, written at camp that night). The rescue as he lived it: Scout licking his face, Ranger Morales's radio ("Found. Both boys. Both okay."), the walk down, his mother's voice on the phone. What he felt, why he ran after the cap, what he learned from Avrumi. He knows only what he saw. *Hook: "There is one thing I still don't get. How did they know to look on the west side of the ridge?"*
9. **Meanwhile, at Camp** (third person: Reb Akiva at camp, the same night). The head count at the trailhead: two missing. The calls; Rabbi Weiss; the rangers; the whole camp saying Tehillim in the dining room. Nosson remembers the red cap blowing west, and that sends the search to the west side. The three flashes were the rangers' signal; a searcher heard faint whistle blasts. At dawn the drone, flown by a ranger with Dovid watching the screen, spots the stone arrow. Then the camp newspaper's article, *Two Campers Found!*, by a boy who was asleep in his bunk all night. *Hook: at the campfire, the editor stands up: "Tonight, the two lost campers will tell us what really happened." Everyone turns to Tzvi Hirsch.*
10. **Two Sides of One Story.** At the campfire, Tzvi Hirsch tells it straight: the cap, the running, the mistake, and the boy who said "Stop." Avrumi gives Tzvi Hirsch credit too: he admitted he was scared, and he stayed. The article's mistake is fixed: they did not wander for miles; they stayed put, and that is why they were found. Reb Akiva and Ranger Morales say the safety rule out loud for the whole camp. Scout brings back the red cap, found on the slope. On Friday Tzvi Hirsch's parents send a box of the bakery's best rugelach for the whole camp's oneg Shabbos. Avrumi draws a new map of Ridge Trail and marks one spot: *Where we stayed put.* THE END.

**Unit fit:**
- **Unit 4.4, how it happened** (ch. 1–3): the order of events, cause and effect (the storm grows, the cap blows away, the boys get lost), how a thunderstorm forms, and problem and solution (lightning safety).
- **Unit 4.5, the reading coach** (ch. 4–7): predict with clues, then check (ch. 4, 6); ask a good question (ch. 5); clarify a confusing part (ch. 6: "cairn", "ledge", "signal"); summarize (ch. 7); all four together.
- **Unit 4.6, who's telling** (ch. 8–10): first person or third (ch. 8 against ch. 7); what the narrator knows and does not (ch. 8); firsthand or secondhand (the notebook against *The Pine Lake Ripple*); comparing two accounts of one event (ch. 9 against ch. 8, and ch. 10).
