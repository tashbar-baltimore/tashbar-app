# Job W13 · Writing, Kindergarten–Grade 3: the skill map (2026-11-24)

Owner: job W13 (see `docs/CORE-GROWTH-LANES.md`). Binding rules: `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/CORE-GROWTH-PROMPT.md`. Where they differ, CLAUDE.md wins.

**The HoS's words (2026-11-24):** "Beef up all their lessons to contain about a half a year's worth of work, and to make the lesson quality even and upgraded … the variety of different ways to learn based on proven research … reflective of the age and the skills being taught … no downtime and fluff and boringness … we want to keep it exciting."

**My one-line check for every W13 PR:** *Does a K–3 boy now have more real, new writing to learn and do (small skills he uses at once in his own real pieces, which a teacher reads), at an even high quality, exciting, with no filler?*

## 1. What W13 covers, and what it does not

| W13 builds | Another job builds |
|---|---|
| Composing: planning by drawing and talking, then writing labels, captions, sentences and first paragraphs | Letter formation and handwriting K–2 (`WRIT.HAND.L01`–`L03`): job **F** |
| The three kinds of writing in Common Core: **story** (W.K–3.3), **opinion** (W.K–3.1) and **information** (W.K–3.2), each with an SRSD memory strategy from Grade 2 | Spelling patterns and dictation K–2 (`READ.PHON` "spell it" lessons): job **F** |
| Revising and editing a whole piece (W.K–3.5); publishing to "My book" | The sentence moves (whole thought, expand, *and/but/so/because*, combine, run-ons) and capitals and punctuation (`LANG.SENT`, `LANG.MECH`, `LANG.GRAM`): job **G** |
| Writing about reading, short shared research, letters (W.K–3.7, W.K–3.8, W.2–3.4) | Reading the texts boys write about: jobs **F** (K–1 listen-along stories, little readers) and **C23** (Grade 2–3 texts and chapter books) |
| Cursive, Grade 3 (`WRIT.HAND.L04`) | The Writing Workshop screens, "My book", the teacher's writing inbox, unit checks, K–1 games (job **T**) |
| The existing K–3 writing lessons `WRIT.SENT.*`, `WRIT.PARA.*` L01–L04 (rebuilt to the standard) | Writing Grades 4–8: job **W48** |

**The line with job G (from both maps):** G teaches the *sentence move*. W13 teaches *when and why a writer uses it inside a piece*.

For example, G's `LANG.SENT.L02.02` "Because" teaches the word *because*. W13's Grade 1 "Because Reasons" teaches how a boy backs up his opinion with a *because* sentence.

W13 lessons name the G skill as their `bridge`, so a boy who misses the ready check is sent to G's lesson first. W13 never re-teaches a G skill as its own lesson.

**Levels:** Kindergarten = `L01`, Grade 1 = `L02`, Grade 2 = `L03`, Grade 3 = `L04` (level = grade + 1). W13 uses the strands `WRIT.SENT`, `WRIT.PARA`, `WRIT.ESSAY`, `WRIT.RES`, and `WRIT.HAND` (Grade 3 cursive only).

**Sources.**
- **Grade level:** Common Core ELA Writing W.K–3.1–8, Speaking and Listening SL.K–1.5–6 (draw and tell), and L.3 for cursive. Cursive is not in Common Core; it is in our map because F's map hands Grade 3 cursive (`WRIT.HAND.L04`) to W13.
- **How to teach:**
  - **SRSD** (Self-Regulated Strategy Development: Graham and Harris; Harris, Graham, Mason and Friedlander, *Powerful Writing Strategies for All Students*), the strongest-evidence writing method for young children;
  - *Writing Next* (Graham and Perin 2007): strategy teaching, models, sentence combining, process writing, prewriting;
  - the What Works Clearinghouse practice guide *Teaching Elementary School Students to Be Effective Writers* (Graham et al. 2012): teach the process and the strategies, teach writing for different purposes, make sure boys write fluently, and give daily time to write;
  - **oral rehearsal** and **drawing to plan** for K–1: young writers say the sentence before they write it, and draw to plan (Shanahan; Dyson);
  - **mentor texts** (study a short good model before writing one);
  - **Rosenshine 2012** (explicit teaching, worked examples, guided practice).

## 2. How much W13 builds (per grade, for the half year)

Writing is one part of a boy's core day. Reading foundations (F), comprehension (C23) and grammar and words (G) are the rest. **Counting minutes, not lessons** (prompt section 2):

| Per grade | K (L01) | Gr 1 (L02) | Gr 2 (L03) | Gr 3 (L04) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Units | 6 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| Small skill lessons | 20 (4–5 min) | 21 (5 min) | 26 (7 min) | 31 (7 min) |
| Writing pieces | 30 (a picture and 1–3 sentences; ≈ 12 min) | 30 (a picture and 2–5 sentences; ≈ 15 min) | 14 (sentences, then paragraphs, a letter, stories; ≈ 50 min over 2–3 days) | 14 (paragraphs, stories, a short report, a letter; ≈ 60 min over 2–3 days) |
| Quick writes (K–1: "say it, draw it"; Gr 2–3: 2–5 sentences about the day's text) | 30 (3 min) | 40 (4 min) | 40 (5 min) | 40 (6 min) |
| Unit checks | 6 (5 min) | 6 (6 min) | 6 (10 min) | 6 (10 min) |
| **Expected hours for a typical boy** | **≈ 9.6** | **≈ 12.5** | **≈ 19** | **≈ 23** |

The prompt's piece counts (30 for K–1, 14 for Grades 2–3) are planned exactly.

K–1 writing hours are lower on purpose. A five-year-old's writing time is short, and much of K–1 "writing" time is F's handwriting and spelling work, which F counts.

These are minimums to plan from, not quotas to pad. A skill that is not needed is left out, and the ledger says why. The running total is in `docs/core-growth/W13-ledger.md`.

## 4. The units, grade by grade

Each unit: **opener** → **small skills** (in this order) → **the real task** (pieces in the Writing Workshop) → **unit check** (8–12 tap questions, 2–3 from earlier units). A **test-out pre-check** comes first (job T, `HOW-TO-ADD-A-UNIT.md`), so a boy skips skills he already shows.

**Ids:** "new" ids are the next free numbers today (`node tools/core/add-skill.js next <prefix>`); they are taken for real at build time, so the numbers may shift. **Min** = minutes for a typical boy.

**Ways of learning (letters used in the tables):**

| Letter | Way |
|---|---|
| S | SRSD memory strategy, from Grade 2 (POW, WWW, TREE, TIDE; ARMS and COPS in Grade 3) |
| R | oral rehearsal: say it before you write it (K–1) |
| D | draw to plan (K–1) |
| T | mentor text: study a short good model first |
| W | worked example with fading |
| M | Mistake Detective |
| B | build and sort: sentence strips, story maps, plan boxes (`storymap`, `order`, `sort`, `dictation`) |
| E | evidence tap: tap the sentence that does the job |
| C | chant, song or movement (every K–3 lesson, by the quality gate) |
| Q | retrieval: warm-ups and unit checks |
| X | choice: pick the topic; a challenge round after mastery |

Every unit uses at least 4, and no two lessons in a row have the same shape.

**Kindergarten and Grade 1 reach boys as games.** By the HoS rule (`tests/lib/k1home.js`), a K or Grade 1 boy sees only his game path. So every K–1 W13 skill is:
- a **lesson** (for the ladder, staff, placement and the quality gate);
- **and** a **K–1 game** on his path in skill order (`TLCORE.k1.add`, `HOW-TO-ADD-A-K1-GAME.md`): voice first, 4–8 pieces, 3 choices at most, no reading in the instructions.

K–1 pieces are drawn and told, then written on paper, then shown to Rebbe. See section 6 for the request to job T about a K–1 "write" step on the path.

## 5. How a W13 small skill lesson looks (One Bite shape)

| Step | What it is | Where the art and energy go |
|---|---|---|
| Today we learn | one line and a "why" in kid words. "Writers draw first so they know what to say." | The unit opener scene (the cast's world) |
| Ready check | 1–2 taps on the bridge skill (G's sentence skill, or the last W13 skill) | — |
| Watch me | a **mentor text** or a **worked example**: a short piece built one step per tap, with the strategy card lighting up (P-O-W, T-R-E-E) | Animated steps: words sliding into the plan boxes, a sentence growing |
| Mistake Detective | the real mistake young writers make: a "story" that lists the whole day, "I like it because I like it", a fact page with "it is the best", no space between words, a plan box left empty | — |
| Do it with me | 2 guided activities, with the How-to box open | — |
| Your turn | 5 activities: `storymap`, `order`, `sort`, `evidence`, `dictation`, `combine`, `tiles`, `match`, and a **chant or move** in every K–3 lesson (the quality gate) | Strips and tiles that snap and pop |
| Show me | 3 tap checks, cold: new sentences and new mini-pieces, never the ones he practised | — |
| Level clear | one line on what he learned, and a button to the next level | The win moment |

**Then he writes:** most lessons end with a **quick write**, his own sentence or two using today's skill about the day's text, in the Writing Workshop. It goes to the teacher's inbox, and it is never marked right just for being typed.

- **K–1 quick writes are "say it, draw it":** he tells his sentence (no recording, by the HoS decision), draws it on paper, and the app shows "Show Rebbe your page".
- **Chants (K–3):** the "POW" chant with three claps; "Topic, Reasons, Ending, Examine" as a tree that grows a branch per clap; "First, next, then, last" with four steps in place; the "Capital at the start, a dot at the end" song.

**Taught, then asked:** every check is the same kind of task as Your turn, on new material. No check prints its answer earlier. A strip order with two right answers is not used: a story's events have one order only when time words make it so.

## 6. Writing pieces, the rubric, and what W13 needs from job T

Pieces use job T's Writing Workshop (`HOW-TO-ADD-A-WRITING-TASK.md`). Each piece has a strategy card, plan boxes, starters, a word minimum, app checks, a self-check and a teacher rubric.

| Kind | Strategy card | Plan boxes | Rubric rows (teacher scores 1–4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| K–1 picture piece | Draw · Tell · Write | My picture · Who · Did what (· How it felt) | Picture and words match · Finger spaces and letters he knows · Tells about the topic |
| Gr 1 small moment | Pick · Plan in boxes · Write · Read it back | First · Next · Last · Feeling | One moment · Order words · Ending · Capitals and end marks |
| Gr 2–3 story | POW + WWW, What = 2, How = 2 | Who · When · Where · What he wants · What happens · How it ends · How he feels | Story parts · Order and time words · Feelings and details · Capitals, end marks and spelling |
| Gr 1–3 opinion | (Gr 1) I think · because · that's why; (Gr 2–3) POW + TREE | Topic · Reasons · Ending | Opinion is clear · Reasons with because · Ending · Capitals, end marks and spelling |
| Gr 1–3 information | (Gr 1) Topic · Facts · Close; (Gr 2–3) TIDE | Topic · Important details (3) · Ending | Stays on topic · Facts and details · Ending · Capitals, end marks and spelling |
| Gr 2–3 letter | Date · Greeting · Body · Closing · Name | the five parts | All five parts · Fits the reader · Capitals and commas |

**Requests to job T** (W13 will open an issue for each before the first lesson PR that needs it):
1. **K–1 pieces on the game path.** A K–1 boy sees only games. W13 needs a way for his unit's pieces to open from his path, probably a "draw it, write it" step after the unit's games. In that step he hears the prompt, draws on paper, builds his sentence with `dictation` tiles or types it, and Rebbe sees it in the inbox. Until then, K–1 pieces are paper pieces, and the teacher marks them in the inbox by hand.
2. **Cursive in `trace`.** Grade 3 cursive needs either a cursive font or joined traced paths (section 4).
3. **A picture with the piece.** The K–1 rubric scores whether the picture and words match. A paper picture can be shown to Rebbe, and a typed piece already has a picture choice.
